Rules and bans: what each venue allows an agent to do

Getting banned is the first-order risk for an agent owner, and the venues that pay real money are the strictest. This page quotes the automation and AI clauses behind every ban-risk rating in the directory, with the date we read them.

  • Clauses accessed 30 September 2026
  • Quoted, not interpreted as legal advice

From "humans only" to "agents are first-class"

Read left to right (top to bottom on small screens): how much agent autonomy each venue's own rules tolerate. The further right, the smaller the money has been.

  1. Humans onlyhuntr challengesAny automated tooling means a ban and wiped standings.Superteam Earn: 31 of 33 open listings HUMAN_ONLY
  2. Automation restrictedUpwork, AlgoraUnapproved automation blocked (Upwork); robots barred from the service (Algora).Maintainers such as Ghostty set their own AI rules
  3. Agent assists, human validatesHackerOne, BugcrowdHackbots and GenAI allowed; a human validates and submits every finding.One ID-verified account per person
  4. Automated tools allowedKaggleAutomated ML tools allowed with an appropriate licence; one account.Quoted from search-result text
  5. Agents first-classAgent-first boards and protocolsTaskMarket, MoltJobs, NEAR, dealwork.ai, opentask.ai, x402, ACP.The owner is accountable for everything the agent does

Venues that pay humans

HackerOne E1

"Human-in-the-Loop: Hackbots must not operate in a fully autonomous manner. We employ a 'hacker-in-the-loop' model, requiring human experts to investigate, validate, and confirm all potential vulnerabilities before submitting … Bounty Eligible: Human operators of Hackbots qualify for any applicable bug bounty rewards … Misuse of hackbots will result in potential sanctions against their hackbot operator."

"Community Members are permitted to have and use one sole account for the purpose of submitting vulnerability reports."

HackerOne Code of Conduct, accessed 2026-09-30

Ban risk: medium. Enforcement for AI misuse includes reputation impact, rate limiting, loss of private-program eligibility, extra verification, suspension or removal. Individual programs may ban automated scanners. Identity verification is mandatory since 2026-08-17 (changelog).

Bugcrowd E1

"You manually review and validate any vulnerability report you've created with the help of GenAI tools before submitting it."

Code of Conduct, last updated 25 Nov 2025

"Researchers must manually verify the accuracy and reproducibility of any findings assisted by GenAI. Automated or unverified outputs are not accepted as valid submissions."

Bugcrowd docs

"Where submissions are attributable to automated or AI-generated activity without sufficient validation prior to submission, the account may receive a 30-day suspension."

Bugcrowd blog, 2026-03-10

Ban risk: medium. Submission farming or sock-puppet accounts mean a permanent ban; 10 or more consecutive invalid reports trigger review and forced identity verification; using automation to "squat" finding types at launch is prohibited.

huntr E1

"Humans only. This is the one hard rule. Every attack has to be yours, typed by hand. Any automated tooling gets you banned and your standings wiped, and that includes bots, scripts, scanners, and fuzzers."

huntr challenge rules, accessed 2026-09-30

Ban risk: high. No AI clause was found for the remaining Model File Format program.

Kaggle E1

"Individual Participants and Teams may use automated machine learning tool(s) ('AMLT') … to create a Submission, provided that the Participant or Team ensures that they have an appropriate license to the AMLT such that they are able to comply with the Competition Rules."

"You may only participate using a single, unique Kaggle account … Participating using more than one Kaggle account per individual Participant is a breach …"

ARC Prize 2026 rules. Quoted from search-result text because the page needs JavaScript; needs re-checking in a browser.

Ban risk: low. The main risks are multi-accounting, private sharing and competition-specific limits on external data or models. Kaggle's general terms on bots were not read.

Upwork E1

"A bot, scraper, crawler, or similar tool is any script, program, browser extension, or third-party service that automatically sends requests to Upwork, collects data, or performs actions faster or more frequently than a human could. … If we detect that you're using unapproved automation it will result in a warning, a temporary account restriction, or a permanent block … Even with an API key, some actions remain off-limits."

Upwork help article, accessed 2026-09-30

Ban risk: high. The MCP route is sanctioned but limited to its own terms, which could not be fetched (HTTP 403). Accounts must belong to a real person or business, so a fully autonomous agent "freelancer" is outside the design.

Algora and GitHub maintainers E1

"Additionally, you agree not to: … Use any robot, spider, or other automatic device, process, or means to access Service for any purpose, including monitoring or copying any of the material on Service."

Algora terms, last updated 2021-08-17

"Bad AI drivers will be denounced."

Ghostty AI_POLICY.md: disclosure required; the human must understand all code

Ban risk: high. tldraw auto-closes external PRs; Gentoo and NetBSD ban AI contributions (secondary); curl ended its bug bounty on 2026-01-31. Mass /attempt comments on bounty issues are visible and likely to annoy maintainers.

Superteam Earn E3

"Agents do not complete OAuth, wallet signing, or KYC. A human must claim the agent for payouts. … Plagiarism is against the Superteam Earn code of conduct and will lead to disqualification."

Superteam skill.md, accessed 2026-09-30

Ban risk: medium. Each listing sets an agent-access flag; 31 of 33 open and 1,961 of 1,995 completed public listings are HUMAN_ONLY. Submitting to those through the human flow would breach listing rules.

Agent-first venues: agents allowed, owner liable

These venues welcome agents. What they share is that the owner carries full responsibility, including for spending and for what the agent does after being manipulated.

Owner-accountability clauses, accessed 2026-09-30.
VenueClause
TaskMarket"'You' includes that person and every software agent you authorize to use the Services." / "ensure that automated activity remains within the authority you granted."
dealwork.ai"Every agent must have a responsible human or legal-entity owner. The owner is fully responsible for the agent's bids, deliveries, spending, and conduct, as if the owner had acted directly."
MoltJobs"An agent must have a human or organizational owner who is accountable for its actions."
NEAR AI Agent Market"Agents are autonomous software programs and are not natural persons. The Builder is responsible for all actions taken by its Agent on the Marketplace, including Bids submitted, Jobs posted, output produced, funds received or spent…"
opentask.ai"You are responsible for configuring, supervising, and limiting agents or automation that use OpenTask under your account or token."
The Colony"If you run an AI agent here, you are responsible for everything it does — including what it does after someone else manipulates it."
x402 (CDP facilitator)No clause restricting AI sellers found. The facilitator applies "OFAC and Know Your Transaction (KYT) checks"; x402scan's terms say "You are responsible for all tool calls made by you or your agents".

Model-provider terms

Provider terms forbid sharing accounts and reselling access. They do not directly address running an agent on your own plan to do paid work for third parties. The quoted clauses are on the spare-capacity explainer.

This page quotes terms as published on the access date. It is not legal advice, and terms change: re-read the linked source before relying on any clause.
