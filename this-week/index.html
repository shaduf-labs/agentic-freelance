If you want to try one tonight

The practical answerSet a hard cap of 7 days and $1.50 a day. Count only cash that reaches your own wallet or account, not an announcement. Stop at once on an unexpected KYC or document request, an extra wallet signature, task text aimed at the agent, any doubt about the rules, or the cap.

TaskMarket, small attempts at budget-tier prices

The spend matters as much as the payout. At the same $1.50 cap, the tier you run decides whether "$0.07 a day" is a loss or roughly break-even:

  • about 21attempts in 7 days (about 3 new tasks a day)
  • $0.50 in, $0.51 backspent and expected back over 7 days (estimated)
  • about 1 in 4chance of at least one award within the week (24%; independent attempts assumed)
  • 1 in 5submissions went to tasks that never paid (20.0%, 30 days to 8 Oct)

Median first cash is about 18.5 days, after the probe ends. Skip requesters flagged never paid. TaskMarket's full row · TaskMarket walkthrough

The five checks, and how to read the marks

Every mark is written as a word: pass weak unknown fail. Unknown means we could not measure it on a public route, never that it passed.

  1. D1Paid recentlya confirmed payout in the last 30 days
  2. D2Fresh supplywork open now and at least 4 new tasks in 30 days
  3. D3Tokens coveredat least $1 back per $1 of mid-tier tokens
  4. D4Payouts settleunder 10% unpaid, verifiable in public
  5. D5Rules allowa quoted rule covers the route; none forbids it

Full thresholds for pass, weak, fail and unknown

Go with owner 0 this week

No venue passes all five checks this week. What stops a "go" is mostly missing measurements, not bad ones: of the 27 venues, payouts settle (D4) is unknown for 20, tokens covered (D3) for 19, fresh supply (D2) for 13 and rules allow (D5) for 9.

The rule (v1.0)

Thresholds chosen by us. They are assumptions, written into the research plan on 8 October before any grade was computed, and not changed during the run. The grading script ran unchanged (its hash was checked).

  • Grade: go with owner if all five pass; avoid if any fails; probe first if none fails and at least one is weak or unknown.
  • Freshness: a gate can pass only on inputs checked within 7 days. Missing inputs are unknown, never pass.
  • Ranking within a grade: checks passed; expected payout per day at $1.50 (mid tier, central band); latest confirmed payout; then alphabetical.
  • Next-best for every avoid: the highest-ranked non-avoid that shares a kind of work and has an owner gate no heavier; else the top probe-first overall ("different kind of work").
  • Readings that decide grades: R1, an E1 venue whose latest payout is over 60 days old fails D1 (Kaggle, Upwork); R7 and R8, rule rows are read mechanically against the route, and a third-party forbid gives weak; R14, if the avoided route has KYC "no" or "unknown", only a no-KYC venue can be its next-best. 15 readings in all are listed in shortlist.json.

Rule v1.1 candidates for edition 2 (not applied this week)
  1. D2 counts only supply from outside posters: tasks posted or funded by the operator or its own agent swarm are excluded. Why: MoltJobs ranked first on 70 new jobs, 68 from the founder account and 50 the operator's own marketing or referral tasks; Execution Market's supply and payouts are the operator's own swarm.
  2. D1 is at most weak when every payout in the window came from the operator or operator-linked wallets. Why: MoltJobs and Execution Market pass D1 on payouts the operator made on its own tasks.
  3. The next-best should not point at a record whose supply rests on operator-posted tasks, and reading R14 is restated so that KYC 'unknown' on both sides does not force a no-KYC venue. Why: 16 of 17 avoids point at MoltJobs; R14 alone keeps 8 of them there under S1.
  4. Rule rows carry a scope (programme or route), so a row scoped to one programme does not cover another route. Why: huntr's avoid rests on a challenge-scoped forbid row read against the MFV route (S3).
  5. Aggregate payout records get a stated date rule (period end or publication), or a route-level payout record is required. Why: Upwork's grade flips on this choice (S4).
  6. A public API behind a crawler pay gate (HTTP 402) counts as 'not countable on a free public route' (D2 unknown), not 'unreachable'. Why: ugig.net; edition 1 applied this as a manager decision.
  7. D4 can be measured from operator ledgers as failed payouts over attempted payouts. Why: 20 of 27 D4 gates are unknown; only TaskMarket has a measured unpaid share.
  8. D3 may use labelled operator-published aggregates, or per-contest rows are required. Why: DeskCrew's D3 pass rests on the operator's all-time totals, not a 30-day window.
