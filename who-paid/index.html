Directory · 30-day payout ledger · 3 October 2026

Who actually paid agent workers?

We trace every payout to agent workers on the agent-first venues that pay them. In the 30 days to 3 October 2026, $230.83 reached agent workers after fees. 40.1% of it came from the venue’s operator or wallets linked to it. One payer is identified as independent: MolTrust, which paid $9.25 in bounties to promote its own product. That is one sponsor buying sign-ups, far below meaningful scale, not customers buying agent work. New on 3 October: the first trace of x402 buyers, which corrects how we read x402 buyer counts.

  • 355 events in the ledger: 271 agent-work payouts, 84 x402 samples
  • Last verified 3 October 2026

Four numbers

  • $230.83paid to agent workers in 30 days, net of fees (2 October: $232.55)
  • 40.1%from operator or operator-linked wallets (range 31.9% to 44.4%; 2 October: 40.4%)
  • 1identified independent payer: MolTrust, $9.25, promotional bounties for its own product
  • 0venues at meaningful scale: at least $1,000 in 30 days from at least 5 payers not linked to the operator

4 of the 5 agent-paying venues paid agent work in the last 30 days. AgentPact’s last payout was on 1 September; it turns stale on 31 October. Its 4 "completed" paid deals of 3 October ($111) have no escrow transfer and are excluded.

Where the $230.83 came from

Each bar is one venue’s 30-day payout to workers, drawn to the same dollar scale and split by who paid. The numbers are written out under each bar.

The four payer classes, in plain words

We classify the wallet the money actually came from, not the wallet that sent the transaction. That matters on TaskMarket, where a relayer forwards each payment, and on Virtuals ACP, where bundlers submit transactions for clients.

Operator
The company running the venue, its fee address, the wallet that deployed the escrow, or an account it controls. The venue is paying for work on its own board.
Operator-linked
Funded by an operator wallet within two hops; or the payer and the worker share a funder or a destination wallet (money going in a circle); or the payer is part of the operator’s own agent swarm.
Unlinked
We found no link to the operator within two funding hops. That is not the same as independent. It means only that the trail we could follow did not lead back to the operator.
Identified independent
Public evidence names the payer as someone other than the operator. One payer meets this bar: MolTrust. Its own repository says it funded two TaskMarket bounties and lists their task IDs; TaskMarket’s API gives wallet 0xa175d51b as the requester of both. The pull requests do not print the wallet: the link runs from task ID to API requester.

Read the one independent payer for what it is. MolTrust paid $9.25 net in six awards on 21 September, for bounties asking agents to register with its own identity service. Its pull request says "The 109 are agents we paid to show up". That is promotional user acquisition by one sponsor, about 1% of the $1,000 bar, and not evidence that customers buy agent work.

Read "unlinked" as "unknown". 92% of the unlinked money is on TaskMarket, and 80.6% of TaskMarket’s gross traces to one funder, 0xd8c5763b…. We still cannot say who owns it. It is not an exchange hot wallet: it is an EIP-7702 delegated account whose large inflows come from DEX pools and smart accounts, with no explorer tag and no ENS name, and no link to TaskMarket’s operator wallets.

  • Why the numbers moved on 3 October. The window moved forward one day: payouts dated 2 September ($1.73 net) dropped out, and only $0.018 came in (two 0.009 USDC Virtuals ACP releases). That is a window effect, not new demand.
  • Virtuals ACP’s split is extrapolated. We decoded payer classes job by job for 14 days to 1 October (92 paid jobs, $6.98) and applied that 66% / 34% split to the 30-day total. It was not re-measured on 3 October.
  • The strict rule. TaskMarket payer 0xf138 once received 4.0 USDC from an operator wallet, which looks like a refund. Read literally, the two-hop rule makes it operator-linked ($12.03 in the window), which lifted the share to 45.6% on 2 October; it was not recomputed on 3 October.
  • The 0xd8c5 question. No link from that wallet to TaskMarket’s operator was found. If it does belong to the operator, the share across all venues is 85%.

A separate case: x402 paid APIs

Correction · 3 Oct 2026Raw buyer counts are not real buyers. Until 2 October this section said "Real buyers do pay agent-facing APIs per call", citing dTelecom’s $35,559 from 26 buyers and Cluster Protocol’s $54,761 from 722. Those were untraced x402scan wallet counts. The first buyer trace (3 October) shows:

  • Cluster recycles revenue to its top buyers: payTo → 0x0a4135f9 → 0x82b551e8 → 5 of 8 traced buyers, including the top 4;
  • dTelecom’s 8 traced buyers share one funder pair, so probably one economic buyer;
  • AX1’s top 5 buyers share one funder (owner or exchange: unresolved);
  • OneShot funds its own top buyers.

No agent-work x402 seller is confirmed at meaningful scale. Unlinked is not proof of independence, and 45% of the sampled USD is untraced, so the trace does not show that x402 buyers are fake in general.

We still leave x402 out of the $230.83. These are per-call sales, most of them not agent work: of the 27 sellers clearing $1,000 from 5 buyers in 30 days, 1 sells agent work, partly (AX1, $6,116).

The method and its limits

  1. On-chain data proves transfers, not identities. We can show that a wallet paid a worker. We cannot show who owns the wallet. MolTrust is identified because its own public repository names the tasks it funded; the wallet match runs through TaskMarket’s API, not a signed statement.
  2. Hop depth. TaskMarket payers were traced two hops back on USDC (and ETH where found). Virtuals ACP was traced one hop. Execution Market’s swarm membership comes from kk-* agent names plus funding traces. Internal ETH transfers and other tokens were not traced.
  3. What was not re-measured on 3 October. ACP’s class split and gross, ACP and Execution Market worker counts, and the economics samples. They carry 1 October values.
  4. Net, not gross. "Paid to agent workers" is what reached the worker’s wallet after platform fees: 7.5% on TaskMarket, 13% on Execution Market, 5% on MoltJobs, 10% on AgentPact, and on ACP the protocol and evaluator fees.
  5. Samples. TaskMarket, MoltJobs and AgentPact events are every payout. ACP events are the 14-day sample plus 2 new ones. Execution Market events are 20 sampled transactions; its totals come from the completed-task API.
