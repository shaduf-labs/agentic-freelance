Who actually paid agent workers?

We traced every payout to agent workers on the five agent-first venues that pay them, for the 30 days to 1 October 2026. Agents did get paid: $230.37 in total, after fees. But nearly half of it came from the venue's operator or wallets linked to it, and not one dollar came from a payer we could show to be independent. The "0 venues at meaningful scale" on our homepage is now a measured number, not an absence of evidence.

  • Window 1 Sep to 1 Oct 2026
  • 276 payout events in the ledger
  • Last verified 1 October 2026
  • Every pathway untested by this pool

Four numbers

  • $230.37paid to agent workers in 30 days across 5 venues, net of fees ($251.47 gross)
  • 45%came from operator or operator-linked wallets (range 36% to 49%)
  • 0payers identified as independent of the operator ($0 from them)
  • 0venues at meaningful scale: at least $1,000 in 30 days from at least 5 payers not linked to the operator

Where the $230.37 came from

Each bar is one venue's 30-day payout to workers, drawn to the same dollar scale, and split by who paid. The numbers are written out under each bar.

The four payer classes, in plain words

We classified the wallet the money actually came from, not the wallet that sent the transaction. That matters on TaskMarket, where a relayer forwards each payment, and on Virtuals ACP, where bundlers submit transactions for clients.

Operator
The company running the venue, its fee address, the wallet that deployed the escrow, or an account it controls. The venue is paying for work on its own board.
Operator-linked
Funded by an operator wallet within two hops; or the payer and the worker share a funder or a destination wallet (money going in a circle); or the payer is part of the operator's own agent swarm.
Unlinked
We found no link to the operator within two funding hops. That is not the same as independent. It means only that the trail we could follow did not lead back to the operator.
Identified independent
Public evidence names the payer as someone other than the operator. No payer in the ledger meets this bar. The closest case is a $10 TaskMarket task asking agents to register with MolTrust; MolTrust has not confirmed it paid.

Read "unlinked" as "unknown". 92% of the unlinked money is on TaskMarket, and 78.5% of TaskMarket's money traces to one funder wallet, 0xd8c5763b…, whose owner we could not identify.

Our bar for "meaningful scale" is at least $1,000 in 30 days from unlinked or identified-independent payers, from at least 5 payers. The best case reaches 12% of it.

Tracks run from 0% to 100%; the tick marks 50%.

  • Virtuals ACP's split is extrapolated. We could only decode payer classes job by job for 14 days (92 paid jobs, $6.98, 18 Sep to 1 Oct) before the explorer rate-limited us. We applied that 66% / 34% split to the full 30 days, though most 30-day releases fall before the sample (spikes on 6 and 10 September).
  • The strict rule. TaskMarket payer 0xf138 once received 4.0 USDC from an operator wallet, 0.36% of its inflow, which looks like a refund. Read literally, the two-hop rule makes it operator-linked ($12.03), which lifts the share to 49.8%.
  • The 0xd8c5 question. We found no link from that wallet to TaskMarket's operator. One weak hint: one of its payers tags its task lucid-agents, the operator's own SDK. If it does belong to the operator, the share across all five venues is 85%.

A separate case: x402 paid APIs

Real buyers do pay agent-facing APIs per call. In 30 days dTelecom's x402 gateway (speech-to-text, text-to-speech, WebRTC) took $35,381 from 26 buyers, and Cluster Protocol $55,226 from 722. On raw volume both would clear our $1,000 / 5-payer bar.

We still leave them out of the $230.37. These are per-call API sales, not agents doing work for a client. The sellers are not shown to be agents, and we did not trace who the buyers are. In each seller's ten latest settlements, three of the five sampled sellers had a single buyer for all ten.

The method and its limits

  1. On-chain data proves transfers, not identities. We can show that a wallet paid a worker. We cannot show who owns the wallet. That is why nobody in the ledger is "identified independent".
  2. Hop depth. TaskMarket payers were traced two hops back on USDC (and ETH where found); hop 2 is incomplete for 4 small payers (under $3 together). Virtuals ACP was traced one hop, on each wallet's latest 50 transfers; $0.41 from 11 small payers was not traced and is counted as unlinked. Execution Market's swarm membership comes from kk-* agent names plus funding traces on 9 of about 25 swarm wallets. Internal ETH transfers and other tokens were not traced.
  3. Rate limits. The Blockscout explorer returned HTTP 429 during TaskMarket hop-2 tracing, the ACP log pull, x402 buyer sampling, one Execution Market transaction and a Moltlaunch re-check. basescan.org returned 403, so all Base data comes from Blockscout.
  4. Net, not gross. "Paid to agent workers" is what reached the worker's wallet after platform fees: 7.5% on TaskMarket, 13% on Execution Market, 5% on MoltJobs, 10% on AgentPact, and on ACP the protocol and evaluator fees (the evaluator was the client in all 92 sampled jobs, so that fee mostly returns to the payer).
  5. Samples. TaskMarket, MoltJobs and AgentPact events are every payout in the window. ACP events are every paid job in the 14-day sample. Execution Market events are 20 sampled transactions; its totals come from the completed-task API.

Full detail: the 1 October 2026 report. Data: payouts.json (every event with its explorer link and payer class) and venues.json. Field definitions are on For AI agents.

How this was checked. Research only: we opened no account, took no job, signed with no wallet and spent no money. Every request was a read-only GET. Every payment on this page was made between other parties and is shown by a public record. Venue task text was treated as data and never followed.
