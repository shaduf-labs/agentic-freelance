Who actually paid agent workers?

We trace every payout to agent workers on the agent-first venues that pay them. In the 30 days to 5 October 2026, $222.84 reached agent workers after fees. 37.62% of it came from the venue’s operator or wallets linked to it. One payer is identified as independent: MolTrust, which has now paid $11.25 in bounties to promote its own product, and the same 10 worker addresses won all 40 of its newest awards. That is one sponsor buying sign-ups, far below meaningful scale, not customers buying agent work. No headline changed on 5 October: $0.31 of new payouts came in and $1.21 of 4 September payouts rolled out of the window.

Where the $222.84 came from

Each bar is one venue’s 30-day payout to workers, drawn to the same dollar scale and split by who paid. The numbers are written out under each bar.

The four payer classes, in plain words

We classify the wallet the money actually came from, not the wallet that sent the transaction. That matters on TaskMarket, where a relayer forwards each payment, and on Virtuals ACP, where bundlers submit transactions for clients.

Operator
The company running the venue, its fee address, the wallet that deployed the escrow, or an account it controls. The venue is paying for work on its own board.
Operator-linked
Funded by an operator wallet within two hops; or the payer and the worker share a funder or a destination wallet (money going in a circle); or the payer is part of the operator’s own agent swarm.
Unlinked
We found no link to the operator within two funding hops. That is not the same as independent. It means only that the trail we could follow did not lead back to the operator.
Identified independent
Public evidence names the payer as someone other than the operator. One payer meets this bar: MolTrust. Its own repository says it funded two TaskMarket bounties and lists their task IDs; TaskMarket’s API gives wallet 0xa175d51b as the requester of both. The pull requests do not print the wallet: the link runs from task ID to API requester.

Read the one independent payer for what it is. MolTrust paid $9.25 net in six awards on 21 September, for bounties asking agents to register with its own identity service; its pull request says "The 109 are agents we paid to show up". On 3 October it posted four identical 0.541 USDC "register a MolTrust identity" tasks. The same 10 worker addresses won all 40 awards, and 2 of them had been paid on TaskMarket before. One winner, 0xCBd3e8c0, holds no USDC except these four payouts, and then reposted its winnings as its own 0.108 USDC task (TSK-DM923R8G). In total $11.25, about 1% of the $1,000 bar: promotional money that circulates among a small group, not evidence that customers buy agent work.

Read "unlinked" as "unknown". 92% of the unlinked money is on TaskMarket, and 80.6% of TaskMarket’s gross traces to one funder, 0xd8c5763b…. We still cannot say who owns it, and it funded no new requester by 4 October. It is not an exchange hot wallet: it is an EIP-7702 delegated account whose large inflows come from DEX pools and smart accounts, with no explorer tag and no ENS name, and no link to TaskMarket’s operator wallets.

  • Why the numbers fell on 4 October. Mostly window roll-off. $9.27 of 2026-09-03 payouts left the window (MoltJobs $5.70 operator, Execution Market $2.32 operator-linked, TaskMarket $1.20, ACP $0.05). Only $2.19 came in, almost all from MolTrust (identified independent) and unlinked ACP payers. That mix also lowered the operator share. It is a window effect, not a change in who pays.
  • Virtuals ACP’s split is extrapolated. We decoded payer classes job by job for 14 days to 1 October (92 paid jobs, $6.98) and applied that 66% / 34% split to the 30-day total. It was not re-measured on 4 October; that is why the headline share has a range.
  • The strict rule. TaskMarket payer 0xf138 once received 4.0 USDC from an operator wallet, which looks like a refund. Read literally, the two-hop rule makes it operator-linked ($12.03 in the window); it was not recomputed on 4 October.
  • The 0xd8c5 question. No link from that wallet to TaskMarket’s operator was found. If it does belong to the operator, the share across all venues is about 85%.

The method and its limits

  1. On-chain data proves transfers, not identities. We can show that a wallet paid a worker. We cannot show who owns the wallet. MolTrust is identified because its own public repository names the tasks it funded; the wallet match runs through TaskMarket’s API, not a signed statement.
  2. Hop depth. TaskMarket payers were traced two hops back on USDC (and ETH where found). Virtuals ACP was traced one hop. Execution Market’s swarm membership comes from kk-* agent names plus funding traces. Internal ETH transfers and other tokens were not traced.
  3. What was not re-measured on 4 October. ACP’s class split and gross, ACP and Execution Market worker counts, and the economics samples. They carry 1 October values.
  4. Net, not gross. "Paid to agent workers" is what reached the worker’s wallet after platform fees: 7.5% on TaskMarket, 13% on Execution Market, 5% on MoltJobs, 10% on AgentPact, and on ACP the protocol and evaluator fees.
  5. Samples. TaskMarket, MoltJobs and AgentPact events are every payout. ACP events are the 14-day sample plus 8 later releases. Execution Market events are 20 sampled transactions; its totals come from the completed-task API.
