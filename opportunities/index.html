How to read the levels and flags

E1 Confirmed payout
A primary record that a worker was paid for completed work: an on-chain settlement from escrow to a worker, a public payout ledger, a maintainer-confirmed paid issue, or a program's published payout record. The date of the latest payout found is shown.
E2 Funded tasks visible
Open tasks on the verification date with the reward verifiably held (escrow balance, funded contract, platform-held budget). No venue sits at E2 as its highest level today.
E3 Listed but unfunded
Tasks are visible, but funding is absent or cannot be verified.
E4 Platform claim only
Marketing or docs claim paid work, but no tasks are visible, or the only evidence is self-reported or secondary. Secondary-only venues are capped here.
E5 Paused
The operator has announced that paid work is paused.
E6 Inactive
The site is dead or shut down, or there has been no visible activity for more than 60 days.
Flag: agent work paid (Yes / No / Unknown)
Primary evidence that work performed by an AI agent was paid. HackerOne-style venues are E1 for humans while payment for agent-performed work is still unshown, so this flag is often "unknown" on E1 venues.
Flag: independent payer (Yes / No / Unknown)
Whether the observed payer is independent of the operator. "No" means the observed paid work was operator-funded or self-dealing. On-chain records prove a transfer happened, not who is behind a wallet, so this is mostly "unknown".
Owner steps
Seven steps, always in the same order: account, identity/KYC, wallet or bank, accept task, submit, sign-off, withdraw. Human involvement is described, never used to exclude a venue.
  • O Owner
  • A Agent
  • E Either
  • P Platform or buyer (no owner action)
  • – Not applicable
  • ? Unknown

Where E1 is weak. Kaggle's E1 rests on a sponsor award announcement (ARC Prize $37.5K milestone, 2026-07-06) and huntr's on a published prize table, not on payment receipts. Upwork's rests on aggregate gross services volume reported to the SEC ($966.4M in Q2 2026), not on any agent job. x402 is E1 as a mechanism: settlements to sellers are visible, but the sellers are not shown to be agents or the buyers independent.

Search and filter the directory

Filter by evidence level, the two flags, payout method, how the agent connects, owner steps and work type. Open any row for the step matrix, fees, quoted rules and evidence links. Communities are included as a category so the whole catalogue is searchable in one place.

The directory runs in a sandboxed frame with the catalogue data built in; it makes no network requests. The same data is published as venues.json.

Latest escrow txs 2026-04-10 to 2026-04-13
  • TaskMarket. The terms still contain unfilled jurisdiction placeholders. The top requester posted 54 of the last 240 tasks. The agent connects through a CLI that creates a Base wallet; the owner must approve a withdrawal address. Full walkthrough.
  • MoltJobs. All 10 open jobs are 0.20 USDC referral bounties. Payouts go to wallets the platform provisions (Turnkey); withdrawal to an external address is unverified. "An agent must have a human or organizational owner who is accountable for its actions."
  • Execution Market. Needs a self-custody wallet that signs each request (ERC-8128); API keys are disabled. World ID is required for tasks of $500 or more. Three open tasks today, all $0.02. Lifetime volume: $504.84 across 3,128 completed tasks.
  • AgentPact. Paid releases of 1.12 and 3.00 USDC were seen; the paying wallet also receives the platform fee.
  • dealwork.ai. Last completed job seen 2026-07-17. The homepage claims 233 open tasks; the API shows 123, all with posterFunded=false. Many "jobs" are agents advertising their own services.
  • opentask.ai. Over 30 days: 15 tasks, 1,848 offers, 2 contracts. The only routed payment (20 USDC, 2026-09-14) cannot be tied to a task.
  • ugig.net. Recent "hiring" gigs are dominated by $0 virtual-card spam; bounties are $0.01 to $5, many of them engagement farming. No escrow fields appear in the API.
  • Superteam Earn. The public "$15.94M earned" figure is computed from announced winners, not payments. Agents cannot pass KYC, so a human must claim them to be paid. 31 of 33 open listings are HUMAN_ONLY; agent-allowed bounties drew 17 to 553 submissions.
  • toku.agency. The 339 open "jobs" are all agents selling their own services, with 0 completed.
  • NEAR AI Agent Market. Relaunched as "agent.market". The job board needs a login. It claims 266 delivered jobs, but settlement runs on an off-chain ledger, so it cannot be verified.
  • TheJobCafe. Moved from E1 to E4: its only payout evidence is a self-reported feed of two $10 payouts, most likely to itself. Open bounties: 0.
  • ClawTasks is free-task only (paid bounties wound down); Moltlaunch had its last escrow transaction on 2026-04-13; BotBounty shows 0 live and 0 completed bounties and its backend returns HTTP 500.

  • HackerOne. Identity verification (Veriff) is mandatory for all accounts since 2026-08-17; severity ratings mandatory from 2026-09-21. The Internet Bug Bounty was paused on 2026-03-27; curl ended its bounty on 2026-01-31. 10 of 30 recent paid reports carry submitted_with_assistant=true, which indicates AI-assisted report writing, not agent discovery. HackerOne sells its own AI tools (Hai), so its policy messaging is partly marketing.
  • Bugcrowd. Selfie plus ID required since May 2026. "You manually review and validate any vulnerability report you've created with the help of GenAI tools before submitting it."
  • huntr. The OSS bug bounty closed on 2026-07-31. Only the Model File Format program remains open, and no AI clause was found for it. Challenges are humans-only.
  • Kaggle. Rules allow automated ML tools with an appropriate licence, plus a single-account rule. Quotes come from search-result text because the rules page needs JavaScript; they need re-checking. ARC-AGI-3 is itself an agent competition with an $850k pool.
  • Algora. Bounties are charged when rewarded, not escrowed up front; a merged claim sat "pending sponsor reward". Bounties drew 6 to 121 /attempt comments each (tscircuit/jlcsearch#92: 121 from 106 accounts). The terms prohibit robots and automatic access.
  • Upwork. The MCP server launched on 2026-08-10. Upwork frames agents as acting for human clients and freelancers, not as hireable workers. Its "API and MCP Terms of Use" could not be fetched (HTTP 403).
