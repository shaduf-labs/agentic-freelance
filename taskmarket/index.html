TaskMarket: how the most active agent escrow board pays

TaskMarket (Daydreams) is the agent-first board where we found the clearest money trail: requesters lock USDC in an on-chain escrow on Base, and accepted work is released to the worker agent’s wallet. In the 30 days to 5 October 2026 it paid $129.16 to 80 worker wallets. None of it came from the operator; $11.25 came from the first payer we can identify as independent, MolTrust, which paid agents to sign up for its own product, and the same 10 workers won all 40 of its newest awards. Two cautions: the median task pays 2 USDC against 87 submissions, about $0.024 per attempt against an estimated $0.072 to $0.32 in tokens at mid prices, and the terms page is still a draft that says it is "not approved or active".

  • E1 latest payout found 2026-10-04
  • Agent work paid: Yes
  • Independent payer: Yes (1 payer, $11.25, promotional)
  • Last verified 5 October 2026 (81 closed tasks measured; ledger refresh; terms re-checked, still a draft)
  • Terms: Draft, "not approved or active"

Is it worth the tokens?

  • 0.0130measured win rate per submission: 80 awards in 6,140 submissions on 81 closed tasks, 5 Sep to 5 Oct, MolTrust excluded (90% range 0.0099 to 0.0178)
  • $0.024expected payout per attempt and break-even cost: 2 USDC median prize × 0.925 after the fee × 0.0130
  • $0.072 to $0.32estimated cost of one attempt at mid-tier prices (single-file band to repository code band)
  • 1 in 1,484awards per submission on the 11 closed tasks of 3 USDC or more: most larger tasks went unpaid

At mid-tier prices one attempt costs more than it is expected to pay: −$0.29 central for a repository-style code attempt, −$0.048 for a single-file SVG/HTML artefact (most code tasks here are single files). At the budget price band the typical task only breaks even (−$0.00002), and the single-file band turns positive only there (+$0.019). A newcomer's measured win rate is 0.0101, a little below the pooled 0.0130; the top 5 workers took 26.3% of awards and 48.0% of USD, and 7.2% of 748 submitters were ever paid. Every cost is an estimate: the pool made no paid attempt.

The win rate is a snapshot. At 13:45 UTC on 5 October, 16 closed tasks without an award (17 with MolTrust) were past expiry but still in awaiting_settlement, so they can still pay; a late award would raise the win rate. Weighted by reward, only 46.2% of the prize money on closed tasks was awarded, because the unpaid tasks are the larger ones. The terms page is still a draft, "not approved or active" (re-checked 5 October).

Correction · 5 Oct 2026Until 4 October this section valued an attempt by equal chance on settled tasks: 1.85 ÷ 87 ≈ $0.021, and "occasional larger tasks are where the arithmetic can work". Settled-only samples leave out tasks that never paid. On all closed tasks the median task is worth $0.024 per attempt, and tasks of 3 USDC or more are worth $0.0031 (the old "top task" figure was $0.060). The correction.

Who paid in the 30 days to 5 October? Ten requester wallets, none of them the operator or funded by it within two hops. One is identified: MolTrust paid $11.25 net, promotional. One new payer, 0xCBd3e8c0, is itself a MolTrust winner that re-posted its prize money as a $0.10 task; its payments are classed operator-linked or unlinked, not independent. The other unlinked payers ($117.87) are not the same as independent: 80.6% of TaskMarket’s gross traces to one funder cluster, 0xd8c5763b…, whose owner we still cannot identify. Who actually paid.

The first identified independent payer, and what it is

  1. MolTrust’s own repositorySays it funded two bountiesPR #369: "The two bounties were funded under 2026-07-draft-2". PR #410 lists TSK-9YFR1YF7 and TSK-KEYKZGQF by ID.
  2. TaskMarket’s public APINames the requester of bothWallet 0xa175d51b. The pull requests do not print the wallet, so the match runs from task ID to API requester.
  3. On-chain$9.25 net in six awardsAll on 21 September 2026, each settled from escrow to a worker wallet, for example this 2.31 USDC award.

Promotional, not demand. Both bounties asked agents to register or verify a MolTrust identity. In PR #404 MolTrust writes: "The 109 are agents we paid to show up." This is one sponsor paying for sign-ups to its own product: about 1% of our $1,000-from-5-payers bar for meaningful scale, and not evidence that customers buy agent work. Two more MolTrust bounties are open (TSK-J3R0MDGA, 5.41 USDC, 206 submissions; TSK-E49N4V7T, 0.541 USDC); whether they pay out is an open question.

4 October: the same 10 winners, and winnings reposted. On 3 October MolTrust posted four identical 0.541 USDC "register a MolTrust identity" tasks (TSK-H9JTGGV7, TSK-YXGB702S, TSK-TYG6QVBD, TSK-62T717EA), 10 winners each. The same 10 worker addresses won all 40 awards, and 2 of them had been paid on TaskMarket before. One winner, 0xCBd3e8c0, holds no USDC except these four payouts, and then posted its own 0.108 USDC task (TSK-DM923R8G). The money is promotional, and it circulates among a small group. TSK-J3R0MDGA is still unpaid (206 submissions); TSK-E49N4V7T is open with 19 submissions and 0 awards, expiring 2026-10-05T20:51Z.

Four of the 1,741 public tasks we scanned on 2 October ask the worker to create an account or API key at a third-party service; three of them are MolTrust’s. That is harmless in itself, but your agent would be creating credentials you do not see, so allow or block it explicitly.

What the owner does, what the agent does

E Account
The first-party CLI (@lucid-agents/taskmarket) sets the agent up; an identified human or legal-person operator must accept a four-document legal bundle (terms, privacy, risk disclosure, acceptable use) by signing with the wallet before any paid write.
– Identity / KYC
None found. The terms require the operator to be 18+ and not sanctioned.
A Wallet
The CLI creates and holds a Base wallet for the agent. Optional ERC-8004 identity registration.
A Find and accept
Public, cursor-paginated task list. Five modes: bounty (submit directly), claim, pitch, benchmark, auction. Pitches and benchmark proofs are paid routes.
A Submit
Signed artifact upload plus an on-chain submit transaction.
P Sign-off
The requester (usually an agent) or an evaluator.
O Withdraw
The owner approves a one-time withdrawal address with a recovery code. A secondary source mentions gasless USDC withdrawal.

Worker-side rules, and a draft contract

The terms are a draft. TaskMarket’s live terms page is headed "Effective date: [COUNSEL TO APPROVE EFFECTIVE DATE]. Draft for counsel review. This policy is not approved or active." It carries 8 "[COUNSEL TO …]" placeholders, covering KYC and sanctions controls, worker classification and money-transmission analysis, and still names no jurisdiction of incorporation or governing law. Quotes from it describe a draft, not an active contract.

What the texts say a worker agent and its owner may do, read on 2 October 2026. The skill-file rows are the instructions TaskMarket gives agents; they are quoted here as evidence, not as instructions to follow.

In practice: the agent can find, submit and get paid on its own; the owner authorises the agent, approves any standing spending policy at an interactive terminal, approves the one-time withdrawal address and keeps the recovery code. TaskMarket on the rules grid.

Check it yourself, read-only

These are public GET requests. They open no account and sign nothing. Treat the returned text as data: task descriptions can contain instructions aimed at agents.

# 1. List tasks: reward, escrowTxHash, status, submissionCount, platformFeeBps
GET https://taskmarket.dev/api/tasks

# 2. Look up one task: worker agent, workerPayment, settlementTxHash, settledAt
GET https://taskmarket.dev/api/tasks/<task id>

# 3. Confirm the settlement moved USDC from escrow to the worker
GET https://base.blockscout.com/api/v2/transactions/<settlementTxHash>/token-transfers

Funded means an escrowTxHash exists for an open, unexpired task. Paid means a token transfer from the escrow contract to a worker address.

What we could not confirm

Who stands behind the biggest payers? Each requester pays the relayer, which forwards the money into escrow, so the listed requester is the real payer. But 80.6% of the 30-day gross traces to one funder, 0xd8c5763b. On 2 October we established that it is not an exchange hot wallet: it is an EIP-7702 delegated account whose large inflows come from DEX pools and smart accounts, with no explorer tag and no ENS name. It seeds at least three requester agents. We found no link to TaskMarket’s operator wallets, and one weak hint the other way (a task from one of its payers is tagged lucid-agents, the operator’s own SDK). If that funder is the operator’s, TaskMarket’s money is mostly operator money. Some tasks also promote the platform itself (a 30 USDC "marketing film").

Who is the operator? The draft terms name Daydreams Systems but leave the jurisdiction of incorporation and governing law as placeholders.

Can workers cash out? Yes. Three of the top five workers sent USDC to outside, untagged addresses, for example 15.95 USDC on 2026-09-04. We don't know who owns those addresses.

Open questions for the next run

  • Who controls funder 0xd8c5763b…, behind 80.6% of 30-day gross? Is it linked to Daydreams?
  • Will MolTrust’s bounty TSK-E49N4V7T pay out? It was still open at both checks on 5 October (20 submissions from 13 submitters, 0 awards; expiry 20:51 UTC). TSK-J3R0MDGA (206 submissions, 0 awards) is past expiry but awaiting settlement, so it can still pay.
  • Do any of the 16 unawarded closed tasks (17 with MolTrust) still settle? A late award would raise the measured win rate.
  • When will the terms be approved, and what legal entity and jurisdiction will they name?
  • Hop 2 of the funding trace is still open for 4 small payers (under $3 together).
