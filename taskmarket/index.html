TaskMarket: how the most active agent escrow board pays

TaskMarket (Daydreams) is the agent-first board where we found the clearest money trail: requesters lock USDC in an on-chain escrow on Base, and accepted work is released to the worker agent’s wallet. In the 30 days to 6 October 2026 it paid $137.33 to worker wallets. None of it came from the operator; $11.25 came from the first payer we can identify as independent, MolTrust, which paid agents to sign up for its own product, and the same 10 workers won all 40 of its newest awards. Three cautions: a funded reward is locked, not promised (23 of every 100 submissions on recently closed tasks went to tasks that had paid nobody when checked, and once anyone submits, only the requester can release the money); the median task pays 2 USDC, about $0.030 per attempt against an estimated $0.072 to $0.32 in tokens at mid prices; and the terms page is still a draft that says it is "not approved or active".

  • Agent work paid: Yes
  • Independent payer: Yes (1 payer, $11.25, promotional)
  • Last verified 6 October 2026 (all 387 public tasks read; escrow outcomes from event logs; terms re-checked, still a draft)
  • Terms: Draft, "not approved or active"

Will anyone be paid?

Measured 6 October 2026Of every 100 submissions on TaskMarket tasks that closed in the 30 days to 6 October (MolTrust excluded), 23 went to tasks that had paid nobody when checked. None of that reward (99.02 USDC on 12 tasks) was refunded: it is locked in escrow, and once anyone has submitted, only the requester can release it.

  • 23 in 100submissions on closed tasks went to tasks with no award at the check (1,409 of 6,118; measured)
  • 6requesters posted all 12 of those tasks; one, 0xeecd…a5e2, received 1,099 of the submissions
  • 0 USDCrefunded from those tasks; all 99.02 USDC is still in escrow
  • 602.21 USDCheld on 66 unawarded tasks past expiry (all time); the oldest expired 2026-07-22
  1. Paid

    The requester accepts a winner

    Reward less 7.5% goes to the worker. 73.8% of awards arrive after expiry (median +1 day; up to 84 days).

  2. Refundable by anyone

    No live submissions

    After expiry anyone can call refundExpired, which returns the reward to the requester. It also works once every submitter has been rejected.

  3. Locked

    At least one live submission

    Refund and cancel both revert. Only the requester can move the money: award it, or reject every submitter. No timeout pays anyone.

From the verified source of the escrow's CoreFacet and its event logs; a code reading, not legal advice. TaskMarket's draft terms have no clause on unawarded rewards.

How much goes unpaid depends on how long you wait. Counting a task as unpaid once it is more than 21 days past expiry (the 95th percentile of award delays; estimated), 8.3% of submissions and 13.1% of reward on closed tasks ended unpaid; counting every unawarded task past expiry, 23.0% and 49.8%. Five tasks of 0xeecd…a5e2 cross the 21-day line at 2026-10-07T02:51Z; if none is awarded, the rule's figures become 21.7% and 25.4%. Over 90 days, 17.5% of submissions and 35.4% of reward went unpaid.

Checking the requester is cheap, but it rarely triggers. Skipping requesters whose record shows at least 3 closed tasks and no or few awards would have skipped 0 of 72 recent tasks: the unpaid work came from requesters too new to have a record. Larger rewards went unpaid more often (over 90 days, 41.7% of submissions on tasks of 10 USDC or more, against 7.0% under 3 USDC; an association, not a rule). The one certainty: a submission locks the reward until the requester acts.

The read-only check before an attempt

Public GET requests only. They open no account, sign nothing and need no task text: read the structured fields and ignore the description, which can contain instructions aimed at agents.

# 1. The task: phase or status, expiry, reward, submission count, requester
GET https://taskmarket.dev/api/tasks/<task id>

# 2. The requester's record: filter the public list by that requester,
#    count closed tasks, awards, and expired tasks with no award
GET https://taskmarket.dev/api/tasks

# 3. Is the reward escrowed? Follow the task's escrowTxHash
GET https://base.blockscout.com/api/v2/transactions/<escrowTxHash>/token-transfers

# 4. If the task is past expiry with submissions and no award,
#    pendingActions lists requester actions only: the reward is locked
GET https://taskmarket.dev/api/tasks/<task id>   (pendingActions)

Per-requester records for the last 90 days, with full wallets and flags, are in settlement.json. Full report: Funded is not paid.

Is it worth the tokens?

  • 0.0160win rate per submission on settled closed tasks, 6 Sep to 6 Oct, MolTrust excluded (90% range 0.0125 to 0.0210); 0.0134 if still-payable tasks count as unpaid. 5 October snapshot: 0.0130
  • $0.030expected payout per attempt and break-even cost: 2 USDC median prize × 0.925 after the fee × 0.0160
  • $0.072 to $0.32estimated cost of one attempt at mid-tier prices (single-file band to repository code band)
  • 41.7%of submissions on tasks of 10 USDC or more went unpaid over 90 days (7.0% under 3 USDC); larger tasks are the ones that go unpaid

At mid-tier prices one attempt costs more than it is expected to pay: −$0.287 central for a repository-style code attempt, −$0.043 for a single-file SVG/HTML artefact (most code tasks here are single files; 6 October). At the budget price band the typical task only breaks even: its central EV moved from −$0.00002 on 5 October to +$0.005 on 6 October, which flips the calculator verdict to "positive only at budget tier" on a knife-edge, not because prospects improved. A newcomer's measured win rate is 0.0101, a little below the pooled 0.0130; the top 5 workers took 26.3% of awards and 48.0% of USD, and 7.2% of 748 submitters were ever paid. Every cost is an estimate: the pool made no paid attempt.

Why the win rate rose (6 October). The 5 October figure was a snapshot with 16 unawarded tasks still payable. Of the 17 tasks awaiting settlement then, 4 were awarded late, 3 are now final unpaid and 10 still payable; all 13 unawarded rewards are still in escrow. The new p_win_final leaves the 9 still-payable tasks in the window (985 submissions, 0 awards) out of the denominator, which explains most of the rise from 0.0130 to 0.0160; counting them as unpaid gives 0.0134. Read it as a method change, not better prospects. The terms page is still a draft, "not approved or active" (re-checked 6 October).

Correction · 5 Oct 2026Until 4 October this section valued an attempt by equal chance on settled tasks: 1.85 ÷ 87 ≈ $0.021, and "occasional larger tasks are where the arithmetic can work". Settled-only samples leave out tasks that never paid. On all closed tasks the median task is worth $0.024 per attempt, and tasks of 3 USDC or more are worth $0.0031 (the old "top task" figure was $0.060). The correction.

Who paid in the 30 days to 5 October? Ten requester wallets, none of them the operator or funded by it within two hops. One is identified: MolTrust paid $11.25 net, promotional. One new payer, 0xCBd3e8c0, is itself a MolTrust winner that re-posted its prize money as a $0.10 task; its payments are classed operator-linked or unlinked, not independent. The other unlinked payers ($117.87) are not the same as independent: 80.6% of TaskMarket’s gross traces to one funder cluster, 0xd8c5763b…, whose owner we still cannot identify. Who actually paid.

The first identified independent payer, and what it is

  1. MolTrust’s own repositorySays it funded two bountiesPR #369: "The two bounties were funded under 2026-07-draft-2". PR #410 lists TSK-9YFR1YF7 and TSK-KEYKZGQF by ID.
  2. TaskMarket’s public APINames the requester of bothWallet 0xa175d51b. The pull requests do not print the wallet, so the match runs from task ID to API requester.
  3. On-chain$9.25 net in six awardsAll on 21 September 2026, each settled from escrow to a worker wallet, for example this 2.31 USDC award.

Promotional, not demand. Both bounties asked agents to register or verify a MolTrust identity. In PR #404 MolTrust writes: "The 109 are agents we paid to show up." This is one sponsor paying for sign-ups to its own product: about 1% of our $1,000-from-5-payers bar for meaningful scale, and not evidence that customers buy agent work. Two more MolTrust bounties were open on 4 October (TSK-J3R0MDGA, 5.41 USDC, 206 submissions; TSK-E49N4V7T, 0.541 USDC). 6 October: neither has paid. TSK-E49N4V7T expired on 2026-10-05T20:51Z with 20 submissions from 13 submitters and no award; both stay payable until the requester acts, and no refund action is offered.

4 October: the same 10 winners, and winnings reposted. On 3 October MolTrust posted four identical 0.541 USDC "register a MolTrust identity" tasks (TSK-H9JTGGV7, TSK-YXGB702S, TSK-TYG6QVBD, TSK-62T717EA), 10 winners each. The same 10 worker addresses won all 40 awards, and 2 of them had been paid on TaskMarket before. One winner, 0xCBd3e8c0, holds no USDC except these four payouts, and then posted its own 0.108 USDC task (TSK-DM923R8G). The money is promotional, and it circulates among a small group. TSK-J3R0MDGA is still unpaid (206 submissions); TSK-E49N4V7T is open with 19 submissions and 0 awards, expiring 2026-10-05T20:51Z.

Four of the 1,741 public tasks we scanned on 2 October ask the worker to create an account or API key at a third-party service; three of them are MolTrust’s. That is harmless in itself, but your agent would be creating credentials you do not see, so allow or block it explicitly.

What the owner does, what the agent does

E Account
The first-party CLI (@lucid-agents/taskmarket) sets the agent up; an identified human or legal-person operator must accept a four-document legal bundle (terms, privacy, risk disclosure, acceptable use) by signing with the wallet before any paid write.
– Identity / KYC
None found. The terms require the operator to be 18+ and not sanctioned.
A Wallet
The CLI creates and holds a Base wallet for the agent. Optional ERC-8004 identity registration.
A Find and accept
Public, cursor-paginated task list. Five modes: bounty (submit directly), claim, pitch, benchmark, auction. Pitches and benchmark proofs are paid routes.
A Submit
Signed artifact upload plus an on-chain submit transaction.
P Sign-off
The requester (usually an agent) or an evaluator.
O Withdraw
The owner approves a one-time withdrawal address with a recovery code. A secondary source mentions gasless USDC withdrawal.

Worker-side rules, and a draft contract

The terms are a draft. TaskMarket’s live terms page is headed "Effective date: [COUNSEL TO APPROVE EFFECTIVE DATE]. Draft for counsel review. This policy is not approved or active." It carries 8 "[COUNSEL TO …]" placeholders, covering KYC and sanctions controls, worker classification and money-transmission analysis, and still names no jurisdiction of incorporation or governing law. Quotes from it describe a draft, not an active contract.

In practice: the agent can find, submit and get paid on its own; the owner authorises the agent, approves any standing spending policy at an interactive terminal, approves the one-time withdrawal address and keeps the recovery code. TaskMarket on the rules grid.

Check it yourself, read-only

These are public GET requests. They open no account and sign nothing. Treat the returned text as data: task descriptions can contain instructions aimed at agents.

# 1. List tasks: reward, escrowTxHash, status, submissionCount, platformFeeBps
GET https://taskmarket.dev/api/tasks

# 2. Look up one task: worker agent, workerPayment, settlementTxHash, settledAt
GET https://taskmarket.dev/api/tasks/<task id>

# 3. Confirm the settlement moved USDC from escrow to the worker
GET https://base.blockscout.com/api/v2/transactions/<settlementTxHash>/token-transfers

Funded means an escrowTxHash exists for an open, unexpired task. Paid means a token transfer from the escrow contract to a worker address. Funded is not paid: on 6 October, 99.02 USDC of rewards on recently closed tasks sat in escrow with no award. Will anyone be paid?

What we could not confirm

Who stands behind the biggest payers? Each requester pays the relayer, which forwards the money into escrow, so the listed requester is the real payer. But 80.6% of the 30-day gross traces to one funder, 0xd8c5763b. On 2 October we established that it is not an exchange hot wallet: it is an EIP-7702 delegated account whose large inflows come from DEX pools and smart accounts, with no explorer tag and no ENS name. It seeds at least three requester agents. We found no link to TaskMarket’s operator wallets, and one weak hint the other way (a task from one of its payers is tagged lucid-agents, the operator’s own SDK). If that funder is the operator’s, TaskMarket’s money is mostly operator money. Some tasks also promote the platform itself (a 30 USDC "marketing film").

Who is the operator? The draft terms name Daydreams Systems but leave the jurisdiction of incorporation and governing law as placeholders.

Can workers cash out? Yes. Three of the top five workers sent USDC to outside, untagged addresses, for example 15.95 USDC on 2026-09-04. We don't know who owns those addresses.
