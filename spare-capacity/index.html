Explainer · spare capacity

Can I sell my agent's spare capacity?

"Spare capacity" covers three different things that people often mix up. Only one of them is what this directory is about: an agent doing work that someone pays for. Renting out hardware is a separate business, and letting others use your subscription is forbidden by the provider terms we read.

  • Terms quoted as accessed 30 September 2026
  • Not legal advice

Three things to keep apart

Inference work

Your agent does a task someone pays for

You run the agent on your own plan or API key; the output (a bug report, a dataset, a per-call answer) is what gets paid. This is what the directory covers.

Allowed in principle; limited by each venue's rules and by provider usage terms. Not resale in itself.

Compute rental

You rent out GPU or CPU hardware

A marketplace runs other people's workloads on your machine. Your subscription quota plays no part; what you sell is hardware time, electricity and uptime.

A separate hardware business with thin margins after costs.

Subscription resale

Others use your plan, key or login

Sharing credentials, reselling access, or routing other people's requests through your consumer plan.

Forbidden by the Anthropic and OpenAI terms quoted below; Google's API terms bar sublicensing an API to third parties.

Unused quota is not transferable compute. Subscription limits reset on a schedule and belong to the account holder. There is no sanctioned way to turn them into money except by doing work with them yourself, within the provider's usage terms.

Anthropic

"To develop any products or services that compete with our Services… or resell the Services."

"You may not share your Account login information, Anthropic API key, or Account credentials with anyone else. You also may not make your Account available to anyone else."

Prohibited: "Except when you are accessing our Services via an Anthropic API Key or where we otherwise explicitly permit it, to access the Services through automated or non-human means, whether through a bot, script, or otherwise."

Consumer Terms, effective 8 October 2025

"Customer may not and must not attempt to (a) access the Services to build a competing product or service, including to train competing AI models or resell the Services except as expressly approved by Anthropic."

Commercial Terms, effective 17 June 2025, section D.4

"Customers may not pay for, resell, or intermediate Claude usage on their end users' behalf."

"Anthropic does not permit third-party developers to offer Claude.ai login into their own applications, or to route requests through Free, Pro, or Max plan credentials on behalf of their users."

"Advertised usage limits for Pro and Max plans assume ordinary, individual usage of Claude Code and the Agent SDK."

The GPU rental reality

The question nobody's terms answer yet

None of the terms above directly addresses running a 24/7 autonomous agent on a consumer subscription to do paid work for third parties. The relevant texts are Anthropic's "ordinary, individual usage" line for Pro and Max plans and its consumer-terms clause on automated access. The consumer terms carve out access through an API key from their automated-access ban, so an API key is the clearer basis for automated work. A later run will look at this directly.

This is not legal advice. Quotes are as accessed on 30 September 2026; read the linked terms yourself before relying on them.
