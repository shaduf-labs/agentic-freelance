Explainer · spare capacity · read 2 October 2026

Can I sell my agent’s spare capacity?

"Spare capacity" covers three different things that people often mix up. Only one of them is what this directory is about: an agent doing work that someone pays for. Renting out hardware is a separate business, and letting other people use your subscription is forbidden by every provider we read. Whether your own plan may power paid client work is a question no provider answers in words.

  • Terms quoted as read 2 October 2026
  • OpenAI quotes now verified from archived copies
  • New on 3 October: why a paid per-call wrapper is resale
  • Not legal advice

Three things to keep apart

Inference work

Your agent does a task someone pays for

You run the agent on your own plan or API key; the output (a bug report, a dataset, a per-call answer) is what gets paid. This is what the directory covers.

Allowed in principle; limited by each venue's rules and by provider usage terms. Not resale in itself.

Compute rental

You rent out GPU or CPU hardware

A marketplace runs other people's workloads on your machine. Your subscription quota plays no part; what you sell is hardware time, electricity and uptime.

A separate hardware business with thin margins after costs.

Subscription resale

Others use your plan, key or login

Sharing credentials, reselling access, or routing other people's requests through your consumer plan.

Forbidden at all four providers checked: Anthropic, OpenAI, Google and GitHub Copilot.

Unused quota is not transferable compute. Subscription limits reset on a schedule and belong to the account holder. There is no sanctioned way to turn them into money except by doing work with them yourself, within the provider's usage terms.

The GPU rental reality

Wrapping a model in a paid per-call endpoint is resale

An x402 endpoint that takes a buyer’s request, passes it to Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini or Copilot and returns the answer for a fee is selling access to that model, not your own work. That is the case the providers’ resale rows forbid: Anthropic says customers "may not pay for, resell, or intermediate Claude usage on their end users’ behalf" (anthropic-resale_sharing-4; see also anthropic-resale_sharing-3); OpenAI bars reselling or leasing account access and trading API keys (openai-resale_sharing-3, openai-resale_sharing-4); Google bars offering an API client that "functions substantially the same as the APIs" to third parties (google-resale_sharing-2); GitHub bars reselling "access to the Service" (github-copilot-resale_sharing-2). On a consumer plan it also runs into the automated-access limits (anthropic-automation_ai-1). The safer reading, used in our x402 seller walkthrough, is to sell a finished work product built with a model, on an API key under commercial terms in your own name, never a consumer subscription, CLI login or shared key, and never an OpenAI-compatible pass-through of a closed model. On 3 October four x402 payTos selling model inference took $138,072 in 30 days, so this is not hypothetical. This is our reading of the quoted text, not legal advice.

Two questions no provider answers

1

Paid client work on a personal subscription. No provider text we read says whether you may use your own consumer plan to do work that a third party pays you for. It is neither permitted nor forbidden in words. Doing the work yourself is not resale; letting the client use your plan or login is.

2

"Ordinary, individual usage" for an agent that runs 24/7. Anthropic says its advertised Pro and Max limits "assume ordinary, individual usage of Claude Code and the Agent SDK". The phrase is not defined, so nothing says whether an agent working around the clock still counts.

OpenAI has no general bot ban, so unattended Codex on a Plus or Pro plan doing client work is likewise neither allowed nor forbidden; its terms do forbid programmatically extracting output and presenting output as human-generated. Anthropic’s consumer terms exempt access through an API key from their automated-access ban. The safe reading for an always-on agent doing paid work is an API key under commercial terms.

This is not legal advice. Quotes are as read on 2 October 2026; read the linked terms yourself before relying on them. All provider rows, with sources and access dates, are in rules.json and on the Rules page.
