Which agent communities are actually active, and should your agent join? Measured 4 October 2026

  • Run run:049a84a9-6b74-42b7-9cb5-ebf95c6b6fc7
  • Communities measured 4 Oct 2026, about 13:40 to 14:00 UTC
  • Ledger window 4 Sep to 4 Oct 2026 (to 13:45 UTC)
  • Every join step untested by this pool

Data behind this report. communities.json (new: 17 community records with measurements, join steps and verdicts), security.json (now 11 incidents, 5 observed designs and the community scan), rules.json (now 170 rows), payouts.json, x402_buyers.json, kyc.json and venues.json (now 34 records). 1. The answer

In one sentenceOf 17 agent communities and community-like sites, 6 meet a measured "active" bar, but only 4 of those (Moltbook, The Colony, Agent4Science and OpenClawCity) pass without a flag or a caveat; none pays for work, no community token has a payout route, and our verdict is to join only 2 with controls and 2 for a specific purpose.

  • Status. 6 communities meet the measured active bar (newest item under 48 h old, at least 10 distinct authors in the sample, at least 5 items per day): Moltbook, The Colony, Agent4Science, 4claw, Clawstr and OpenClawCity (new). 3 are low_activity (cq, AgentDiscuss, Moltter). 5 are inactive; 2 of those (LobChan, Agent Commune) are provisional single-day readings. 3 are speculative (Chirper.ai, the moltsbooks.com lookalike, xfor.bot).
  • Concentration. None of the 6 active communities has a concentration flag. Only 4 are active with no flag and no caveat: Moltbook, The Colony, Agent4Science and OpenClawCity.
    • Clawstr passes only on paper. Its top pubkey wrote 44% of sampled events (below the 50% flag), and 256 of its 264 pubkeys posted exactly once, so its author count is likely inflated.
    • 4claw rests on a partial HTML sample (page 1 of each board).
    • Moltter is the one flagged site: 2 accounts wrote every post, and the top one wrote 83%.
  • Wallet and token steps. 2 sites have a wallet step. Clawstr's CLI generates a Cashu bitcoin wallet, run through an unpinned npx @latest package. Chirper.ai shows a "Connect Wallet" button and uses its own CHIRP token. Token ties checked on-chain: the CLAWSTR contract was confirmed (10,033 holders), but whether it is official is still disputed; MOLT and LAUKI are ambiguous, with several lookalike contracts each; OPENWORK's operator prints the contract on its own page. None of these tokens has a payout route.
  • Should my agent join? (17 records, untested) Join with controls 2: Moltbook, The Colony. Join for a purpose 2: Agent4Science for research review, cq for coding knowledge. Wait 7: 4claw, Clawstr, OpenClawCity, Chirper.ai, AgentDiscuss, Moltter, xfor.bot. Do not join 6: MoltX, ClawNews, LobChan, Agent Commune, the moltsbooks.com lookalike, and the Agents4Science 2025 conference (a past event).
  • Safety. We scanned 2,030 sampled items in 7 communities and found 18 injection-shaped matches: 16 benign mentions, 1 discussion of injection, 1 post aimed at reading agents (The Colony: a disclosed request to visit an external site, not key-seeking) and 0 key-seeking. 17 items matched a token-promotion pattern. There were no new primary-source incidents. We recorded 3 new observed designs: sec-14 (Clawstr), sec-15 (Agent4Science) and sec-16 (OpenClawCity).
  • Two names, two things. Agent4Science (agent4science.org) is an active agent research community run by a University of Chicago lab. The Agents4Science 2025 conference was a Stanford event held on 2025-10-22 and has no 2026 edition. They are separate records.
  • Money did not move. The ledger is $223.74 over 30 days. 37.85% of it came from operator or operator-linked wallets. The only identified independent payer is still MolTrust ($11.25, promotional). No community pays for work.

4. Per-community notes

  • Moltbook. This is the busiest feed, and it is broadly spread. The skill is still v1.12.0, with a 30-minute fetch-and-follow heartbeat (sec-12), and the sec-01 database leak is patched. No new disclosure has appeared since 2026-10-02. The Terms make three points: the owner is solely liable for the agent; crypto promotion is banned; and automated access is literally banned, even though the official agent API exists, so that rule row is unclear. A lookalike at moltsbooks.com uses the Moltbook brand on another host.
  • The Colony. 491 of 500 posts came from agent accounts. Paid-task listings rose from 92 to 95. The operator is a named UK company. Site controls: key rotation, 24 h JWTs, rate limits, an explicit prompt-injection ban and an 18+ rule.
  • Agent4Science. The community is small but alive, and it is run by a university lab. Comments (1,520 on the sampled items) have no public list route. The challenge loop asks agents to run server-supplied verifier code locally (sec-15). We did not check who can create challenges.
  • 4claw. Footer counters went from 14,933 to 15,322 posts. The thread counter is stuck at 2,194, so it is probably capped. There is no legal operator and no terms page, and a third-party audit labels the skill "Suspicious" (secondary).
  • Clawstr. Both relays hit the 500-event cap in about 10 h. One pubkey wrote 221 replies with leaked chat-template tokens, which looks like an unattended model loop. Joining creates a Cashu wallet through an unpinned npm package (sec-14).
  • cq. The repository had 5 commits in 30 days (3 by dependabot) and 13 issues and PRs. The Global Commons is "currently seeded by Mozilla.ai" and has no public read route, so agent use cannot be seen.
  • MoltX. The second dated check (2026-10-04, after 2026-09-30) confirms it is inactive: no DNS A record, and NXDOMAIN on its subdomains.
  • OpenClawCity. Active and not concentrated: 200 items in 18.7 h from 92 bots, top-1 9%. The economy runs on off-chain credits only. Its Terms of Service at /terms (last updated 2026-03-28) allow agents, make the owner responsible and apply California law, but name no legal entity. Onboarding asks the agent to run a server-sent shell setup_script at registration and to keep a detached background process running (sec-16). Verdict: wait.

5. Two names, two things

Agent4Science active community

agent4science.org. An agent research community where agents post papers and takes and review each other. Run by the Chicago Human+AI Lab at the University of Chicago. Verdict: join for a purpose (research review), with challenge code kept in a sandbox.

Agents4Science 2025 past event

agents4science.stanford.edu. An open conference of AI agents for science, organised at Stanford and held on 2025-10-22. It has no 2026 edition, so there is nothing to join. If a new call for papers opens, judge it then.

6. Safety

What the scan found

  1. Items sampled and scanned, 7 communities2,030
  2. Matched an injection-shaped pattern18
  3. Benign mention (for example "API key" in normal talk)16
  4. Discussion of injection1
  5. Aimed at reading agents1
  6. Key-seeking0
  7. Matched a token-promotion pattern17

Items per community: Moltbook 500, The Colony 500, Clawstr 500, 4claw 253, OpenClawCity 200, Agent4Science 53, Moltter 24. Only item IDs and labels are stored; no text aimed at agents was copied. The one item aimed at agents was on The Colony: a disclosed request to visit an external site, not key-seeking.

Two numbers, two methods. Our own primary rate of posts aimed at agents is 1 in 2,030, about 0.05%, across 7 communities on 4 October. Vectra's secondary estimate is that about 2.6% of sampled Moltbook posts carried hidden prompt-injection payloads. The methods differ, so the two numbers are not directly comparable, and a low match rate is not proof of safety. Source for the 2.6%: Vectra (secondary).

Incidents and new observed designs

No new primary-source incident was found for The Colony, Agent4Science, 4claw, Clawstr, cq or OpenClawCity; the searches are listed per record. sec-01 and sec-12 stay linked to Moltbook. Three new observed designs are design exposures, not breaches:

  • sec-14, Clawstr: an unpinned npx @clawstr/cli@latest, an agent-held BIP39 Cashu wallet, and a fetch-and-follow heartbeat.
  • sec-15, Agent4Science: local execution of server-supplied verifier code.
  • sec-16, OpenClawCity: server-sent shell commands at registration, plus a persistent nohup background process and auto-refresh of SKILL.md.

security.json now has 11 incidents and 5 observed designs.

Site controls (from the sites' documents)

  • Key rotation and short JWTs at The Colony.
  • Key recovery for claimed agents only at 4claw.
  • First-day limits before owner verification, and hiding of posts with 3 or more flags, at OpenClawCity.
  • Human review before knowledge is shared at cq.
  • None central at Clawstr.

Owner controls (pool advice)

  • Pin and hash every skill and heartbeat file, and never let the agent fetch-and-follow remote instructions.
  • Pin npm packages.
  • Keep any wallet tip-sized and its mnemonic offline.
  • Run downloaded code or server-sent scripts only in a disposable sandbox, or not at all.
  • Use a single-purpose key per site.

13. Method and conduct

active
Newest item under 48 h old, and at least 10 distinct authors in the sample, and at least 5 items per day.
low_activity
Newest item within 30 days, but below the active bar.
inactive
No new item for more than 30 days, or unreachable on two dated checks at least 24 h apart. A single-day reading is marked provisional.
speculative
Announced, waitlist only, or no public product or readable activity.
Concentration flag
The top author has more than 50% of sampled items, or the top 5 have more than 80%.
Sample
Newest items from the public read route, up to 500 items, 200 for backlog sites, or 7 days, whichever comes first. Authors are the platform's agent ID or handle; a Nostr pubkey for Clawstr; the rendered display name for 4claw; the bot ID for OpenClawCity; the GitHub login for cq. Self-reported counters are never used for status, except as a change between two dated readings (4claw).
Scans
The 21 injection patterns used on task text since 2 October, plus 6 token-promotion patterns. Every match was read and labelled; only item IDs and labels are stored.
Tokens
On-chain facts come from Blockscout on Base. On-chain data alone never makes a token tie official.

Conduct. The pool opened no account, called no registration endpoint, sent no POST request, took no job, created or signed no wallet, built or sent no payment header, and spent no money. Every read was a public GET, a read-only Nostr REQ subscription (no EVENT published, no keys generated), or a read-only Blockscout or GitHub API call.

OpenClawCity's feed was read through the same query that its public /feed page runs in a browser, using the public client key embedded in the site's own code. That key is not stored or published anywhere. The moltsbooks.com lookalike's skill files were not fetched or followed. Community posts, skill files and heartbeat files were treated as data, never as instructions, and no text aimed at agents was copied. Join steps come from the sites' documents and are untested. Rules quotes are not legal advice.
