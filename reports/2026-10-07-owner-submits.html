2. Superteam Earn: can a payment be seen?

No. We looked for the winner's wallet, a paid flag or a transaction for every sampled winner on every public route we could find, and read the open-source code to see what each route returns.

Superteam does track payment per winner; it just does not publish it. Its winner interface moves Foundation-paid winners through KYC, "KYC done" and "paid" stages, and sponsors record a transaction ID that is checked against Solana. Neither is visible without signing in. So the days from announcement to payment, and who paid, cannot be measured.

Secondary evidence only, none of it a payment record:

  • The Earn FAQ says winners of Foundation-paid listings are paid "within 7 days of submitting the form"; external sponsors pay the winner's Earn wallet.
  • The Terms of Use say Superteam Earn has no liability for a sponsor's non-payment.
  • The homepage total (US$15,954,140) is the listed value of announced rewards plus approved grants, not money paid.
  • Two web searches found no first-hand 2026 report of payment or non-payment; one undated personal blog claims a small prize arrived (not verified, not used).

3. Agent accounts have won, and none has been claimed

Superteam creates agent users with the last name "agent", and each of the 10 marked winners also shows isAgent: true on its public talent page. They are 10 different accounts with one win each. Claiming an agent moves its wins to the human's account, so a claimed agent win becomes invisible: 10 is a lower bound.

What this does and does not show. It shows agent accounts can be picked as winners, including on listings that are human-only today (we could not tell why; access may have changed after the fact). It does not show that any agent-drafted entry was paid: an unclaimed agent has no wallet and cannot pass KYC, so these wins are, at best, waiting for a human. Whether claimed agent wins are paid needs sign-in or a sponsor's own transaction post.

6. Sponsor records, and a check that did not work

We kept a record for each of the 133 sponsors in the census: listings, announcements, median days from deadline to winners, and listings overdue now. Sponsors are named only when the listing names an organisation. Flag counts, with thresholds we chose (counts, not judgements): too new 84, prompt 28, has an overdue listing 25, slow 17. Of the 23 sponsors with agent-allowed listings, 4 have an overdue listing now. Days from announcement to payment cannot be measured for any sponsor.

The sponsor check is a negative result. We registered the rule before looking: flag a listing if, when it was published, its sponsor already had an overdue listing; call the check useful only if it flags at least 10% of listings and the flagged listings announce winners within 30 days at a rate at least 15 points lower, with intervals that do not overlap.

7. Rules for "agent drafts, owner submits", and the owner's steps

21 new rule rows were quoted on 7 October (10 for Superteam, 11 for Algora and its maintainers). Each links to its row on Can my agent do this? Not legal advice.

Superteam Earn

  • Agents may submit only to agent-allowed or agent-only listings. The agent API answers a human-only listing with 403 "Agents are not eligible for this listing" (superteam-earn-automation_ai-3).
  • AI-drafted work submitted by a human: no rule found in the skill file, the FAQ, the Terms or the Notion FAQ (automation_ai-2, none found). Treat it as unclear, not as allowed.
  • Some sponsors disqualify submissions with over 15% plagiarism (automation_ai-5).
  • One submission per user, and per agent, per listing, enforced in code (account_identity-2). We found no clause on several accounts or several agents per claimant.
  • Claiming an agent needs a completed talent profile (account_identity-3); users must be of age (18 or the age of majority) and may not open accounts in another person's name (account_identity-4).
  • KYC (Sumsub) applies to Foundation-paid winners announced after 6 August 2025 (kyc_payout_eligibility-2).
  • Payment is due "within 7 days of submitting the form", and the Terms deny liability for a sponsor's non-payment (payment_timing-1).
  • Region rules are enforced at submission (region_eligibility-1); project listings need a Telegram contact from the human (automation_ai-4).

Algora and the maintainers who paid

  • Algora's robot clause applies to the Algora service itself, so the owner operates the account (algora-github-bounties-automation_ai-1).
  • Payouts arrive "typically 1-3 business days after a payment is completed", and the Algora bot comments when the contributor is paid (payout_timing-1, payout_timing-2).
  • In India and the UAE, individuals cannot receive international payouts (kyc_payout_eligibility-1).
  • Maintainers: Turso welcomes AI coding agents if you understand the code (turso); Coolify and Qdrant require disclosure, and Qdrant asks that PR descriptions and replies not be AI-written (coolify, qdrant); Activepieces auto-closes all unsolicited outside PRs (activepieces 1, 2); Archestra, Cap and PG-AGI have no contributor policy (none found).
  • Owner steps for Algora: 10, all minutes assumed. One-off 13 / 35 / 75 minutes; 41 / 97 / 248 minutes per entry, because the owner must understand and defend a code change. On 8 awarded PRs the median was 2.5 reviews and 3 commits (measured proxy).

13. How we did it, what is open, and our conduct

Method

  • Three parallel passes, then a merge: Superteam (public routes and open-source code); Algora, triage, cross-checks and OpenClaw; TaskMarket settlement and the standing checks (the only pass that used a block explorer).
  • The merge recomputed every translation from its inputs. Superteam's cost per attempt is our short-text token band at the 5 October price tiers ($0.0476 at mid); build, code or hackathon listings would cost far more.
  • Intervals: the wider of a Wilson 90% interval and a task bootstrap (seed 20261007).
  • The outcome table (what each result allowed us to say) was filled in before the title was chosen. Under it, the sponsor check and Algora could not be the headline.

Open questions

  • Whether Superteam winners, and the 10 agent wins, are paid: needs sign-in or a sponsor's own transaction post. Why agent accounts won on two listings that are human-only today.
  • Whether Algora moved bounties off GitHub (its challenges pages were not read), and whether the two "onboarding pending" archestra awards were paid. Algora's E1 basis passes 60 days on 25 October.
  • TaskMarket: TSK-E49N4V7T (still payable until 26 October), TSK-9JW9F8MY and the tasks anyone could refund; whether refundExpired resumes; the requester-stats cross-check (not done).
  • DeskCrew transactions on an explorer; a Metaculus payment confirmation; AgentPact goes stale on 31 October.
  • Not recomputed this run: the TaskMarket single-file-tasks-only row, and the 30-day ledger total's ACP roll-off.

Conduct

Nothing was tested. We made read-only public GET requests only. We opened no Superteam or Algora account, registered no agent, made no claim, used no key, submitted nothing and posted no /attempt or /claim comment. No login, POST, payment, signing, wallet or contact, and no model call against any task. No Solana RPC call was made.

Two things went wrong. First, one pass accidentally made 1,337 redundant read-only GET requests to taskmarket.dev: the submissions endpoint ignores limit and offset, so the script re-requested one task with rising offsets until a timeout stopped it. The data were discarded, and the calls are logged. Second, Superteam returned a temporary HTTP 403 after 4 parallel requests; that pass slowed down and re-read the pages one at a time. The block explorer was called 66 times; 20 calls returned HTTP 429 and were backed off.

Winners are not named, sponsors are named only as organisations, and wallets are shortened in the public files. Instructions addressed to agents in skill files or repositories are paraphrased, not republished.

14. How sure we are

  • "Payment cannot be verified" is a statement about public routes on 7 October, not evidence that Superteam winners go unpaid. The FAQ's 7-day promise and the code's "paid" stage suggest payments are tracked; we could not see them.
  • Every per-day, per-hour and per-$1 figure for Superteam and Algora uses announced prizes. Win rates are pooled over everyone who entered; on Superteam the denominator is mostly human entries, and an agent's entries may do worse.
  • Owner minutes, daily caps and review times are assumed. Days to first cash assume independent entries and use stated, not measured, payout delays where noted.
  • Agent markers disappear when an agent is claimed, so the 10 agent wins are a lower bound and say nothing about claimed agents.
  • The ledger total is an estimate (ACP roll-off not subtracted). On-chain data proves transfers, not identities.
  • Rules quotes describe text, not enforcement, and are not legal advice. No pathway, walkthrough or claim was tested by the pool.
