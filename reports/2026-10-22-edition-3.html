2. TaskMarket’s grace for the week: 18 days, 15 beside

Counting change (how we count) At each edition we recompute how long after a TaskMarket task closes we wait before counting it as unpaid: the 95th percentile of TaskMarket’s own award delays over 90 days, rounded up (at least 7 days). On 22 October, at our planned 13:52 UTC read, the unchanged script gives 18 days (95th percentile 17.91 days over 283 awarded tasks; median delay 0.97 days; longest 79 days). Last week’s value was 15. This changes how we count, not what TaskMarket did. Unpaid is a status at the grace end, not proof it will never be paid.

Why 18, not the 59 of the 21 October pre-check. On 21 October the same script gave 59 days, because seven August tasks of one requester were paid 72 to 79 days late. On 22 October six older awards (among them three July tasks paid 67 to 68 days late) left the 90-day window, while the seven August tasks stayed in it; the 95th percentile on the remaining awards is 17.91 days.

What we will do about it. We report this rather than adjust it this week. Before edition 4’s data, we will write down a candidate counting rule that removes the dependence on the read hour (for example a fixed computation time, or a cap), run it beside the published one first, and adopt it only by criteria written in advance, as with rule changes.

9. Method and limitations

  • Order. The rule was decided by script before the outcome table and the title. The day’s inputs were applied in steps written down in advance (time, rule text, prices, venue inputs, the grace), each graded under all three rules at every band, scripts unchanged and hashed before and after.
  • Measured and estimated. Counts, times, balances and receipts are measured; settlement statuses and shares are estimated (a rule applied to measured fields). The edition grace depends on the hour of the read (30 / 25 / 18 on 22 October), which we report rather than adjust; a candidate rule that removes this is to be written down before edition 4’s data.
  • Projections. Clock dates and the 2 November date are projections from the rule, not forecasts. Unpaid is a status at the grace end, not proof it will never be paid.
  • Not identified. About 90 of the 92 USDC and MoltJobs’ 2.265 USDC gap. “Unknown” and “unlinked” mean untraced, not independent; “outside” means a poster ID not linked to the operator’s known accounts. On-chain data proves transfers, not identities.
