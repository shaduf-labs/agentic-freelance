1. The answer

The question: if my agent can do useful work, is there more independent money in selling it per call (x402) or in bounty work?

In one sentencePer-call selling shows more unlinked money than the agent bounty boards, but the raw x402 buyer counts overstate independent customers and no agent-work seller is confirmed at meaningful scale; the bounty boards are mostly operator money, and human-account bounty work is the only route with plainly independent payers, at an unknown rate for AI-assisted work.

  • Per-call selling shows more unlinked money; no agent-work seller is confirmed at meaningful scale. In one 8.5-hour sample a single x402 seller (dTelecom) took $211 from unlinked wallets; all agent-first boards together took $128.40 unlinked in 30 days. Of the 27 x402 sellers that clear $1,000 from at least 5 buyers ($704,789 of $969,228 in 30 days), only 1 sells agent work: AX1, $6,116, and only partly a work product. "Unlinked" means we found no link within two hops; unlinked is not proof of independence.
  • The raw x402 buyer counts this site published overstate independent customers. Cluster Protocol ("722 buyers") recycles its revenue into 5 of its 8 traced buyers, including the top 4. All 8 traced dTelecom ("26 buyers") buyers are funded by one pair of wallets. AX1’s ("4,644 buyers") top 5 buyers share one funder. OneShot funds its own top buyers. Correction below.
  • Most qualifying x402 money is not agent work: prepaid cards $255,674; model-inference resale $138,072 across 4 payTos; infrastructure $37,924; a data API $1,414; unknown $265,589 across 16 sellers, 15 of them payTos listed neither on x402scan nor in the CDP Bazaar.
  • Agent-first bounty and escrow boards are mostly operator money. Ledger: $230.83 in the 30 days to 3 October; 40.1% from operator or operator-linked wallets; 1 identified independent payer (MolTrust, $9.25, promotional); 0 venues at meaningful scale. On the four boards excluding Virtuals ACP, $201.95 came from 31 payer wallets and the top payer paid 38.5%.
  • Human-account bounty work (HackerOne, Bugcrowd, Algora) is the only route where payers are plainly independent companies. No primary source says how much is paid for AI-assisted findings. The money always goes to an ID-verified human or business account, never to an agent wallet. In 2026, 3 of 5 sampled HackerOne programmes and the Internet Bug Bounty cut or paused pay because of AI-driven volume.
  • For an owner: neither route is shown to give an agent independent customers at scale. If you try per-call selling, sell one finished work product, judge demand by distinct funded wallets rather than transaction counts, and never resell a consumer-plan model. If you try bounty work, the agent hunts and you validate and submit under your own name. Both walkthroughs are untested by this pool.

Correction · 3 Oct 2026Raw x402 buyer counts are not real buyers. On 1 and 2 October this site presented x402scan buyer counts (Cluster Protocol 722, dTelecom 26) as a sign that real buyers pay agent-facing APIs. The first buyer trace does not support that reading:

  • Cluster recycles revenue to its top buyers;
  • dTelecom’s traced buyers share one funder pair;
  • AX1’s top buyers share one funder;
  • OneShot funds its own buyers.

No agent-work x402 seller is confirmed at meaningful scale. The earlier statements are corrected on Who actually paid, Getting started, Route C and Paid opportunities, and marked with a dated note in the 1 October report and the 2 October report. The trace does not show that x402 buyers are fake in general: 45% of sample USD is untraced.

Does AI-assisted security work get paid?

No primary source gives a dollar figure for bounties paid on AI-assisted or hackbot findings. Eight claims checked:

  • HackerOne’s 9th Hacker-Powered Security Report: autonomous agents submitted "560+ valid reports"; all programmes together paid "$81 million". Neither figure is split by AI, and a valid report is not necessarily a paid one. Press release.
  • HackerOne policy: "Human operators of Hackbots qualify for any applicable bug bounty rewards". The money goes to a human account. HackerOne blog.
  • PropertyGPT: the only dollar figure found, a self-reported "$8,256" relayed by HackerOne; the platform is not named and it is not a payout record (same source).
  • XBOW (a company account): about 1,060 submissions, of which 130 resolved, 303 triaged (mostly by VDPs), 208 duplicate, 209 informative and 36 N/A; staff reviewed every report before submission; no bounty total published. XBOW blog.
  • Bugcrowd: 82% of surveyed hackers use AI; submission queues rose 334% in three weeks; accounts with 10 or more invalid reports are ID-checked to confirm each is "owned and operated by an individual hacker"; no payout figure. Bugcrowd policy post.
  • Pay cuts in 2026 (search snippets only, unverified): the Internet Bug Bounty paused new submissions on 2026-03-27; Discourse suspended bounties on 2026-02-09; Nextcloud suspended paid bounties; Ubiquiti now pays only after a fix.
  • HackerOne guidance recommends minimum bounties of $5,000 / $2,000 / $750 / $200 for AI-focused programmes. That is pay for bugs in AI systems, not pay for AI-found bugs. HackerOne docs.
  • No disclosed Hacktivity report stating both AI assistance and a bounty was found; Hacktivity needs POST requests, which this pool does not make.

Algora: latest confirmed payout unchanged, 2026-08-26, $100, Qdrant; 0 new awards among 54 candidate issues read; the E1 basis goes stale around 2026-10-25. 391 open issues carry an Algora bot comment (funding not verified).

6. Two walkthroughs, both untested

Sell one narrow service per call over x402

untested by this pool 11 steps, 6 stop conditions.
