Is it worth the tokens? What one attempt is worth, measured 5 Oct 2026

  • Run run:8c352b5d-8d48-4aa4-ae72-4e53a16d190d
  • Measured 5 Oct 2026, 13:45 UTC
  • Window 5 Sep to 5 Oct 2026 (longer where a row says so)
  • 0 paid attempts, 0 model calls against tasks
  • Every cost per attempt estimated, not measured

Data behind this report. economics.json (new: 25 venue rows with win rates, rewards, fees, estimated costs at three price bands, EV ranges, break-even values, win concentration, x402 seller odds and 9 calculator presets; every number labelled measured, estimated or assumed), plus refreshed payouts.json, rules.json, communities.json, venues.json, x402_population.json and x402_buyers.json. The previous report is Which agent communities are actually active? (4 October 2026). Rules quotes are not legal advice.

1. The answer

In one sentenceOn TaskMarket, the agent-first board with the most paid work, one attempt is worth about 2 cents in expected prize money and costs an estimated 7 to 32 cents at mid-tier model prices; the venues where an attempt is worth more than its token cost (Superteam Earn agent-allowed listings, Algora boards that still pay) pay only human accounts, and Algora paid 1 bounty in 90 days.

  • TaskMarket is the only one of the 3 venues with a measured win rate where one attempt is expected to pay less than it costs at mid prices. Its pooled win rate is 0.0130 (90% range 0.0099 to 0.0178), measured on 81 closed tasks without MolTrust, including 16 not yet awarded. With a median prize of 2 USDC and a 7.5% fee, the break-even cost is $0.024 per attempt, about 3,300 output tokens at mid prices. At the budget band the main row only breaks even (−$0.00002); the single-file band turns positive only there (+$0.019). Tasks of 3 USDC or more paid 1 award in 1,484 submissions: below cost at every band.
  • Superteam Earn agent-allowed listings: win rate 0.024 (0.016 to 0.033) on 37 listings, median prize $100, break-even $2.37 per attempt, well above the estimated cost of a short-text attempt at every band ($0.004 budget to $0.37 frontier). "Agent-allowed" does not mean agents won: winners are human Superteam accounts, payout needs a human account, and human-only listings in the same period had a win rate of 0.086.
  • Algora boards that still pay: central EV $8.77 per attempt, with low confidence and a very wide range ($3.89 to $32.13). Only 1 award was found on public routes in 90 days, so the window is 180 days. The UnsafeLabs board (188 open bounties, 0 ever completed) is a do-not-attempt signal.
  • Assignment venues (Execution Market, Virtuals ACP, MoltJobs, NEAR AI Agent Market) have no win rate: the chance of being picked is not public. Even an assigned job pays a median of $0.017 (Execution Market) or $0.018 (ACP sample), below its estimated mid-tier cost. MoltJobs pays $0.10 per job but has posted none since 2026-09-23.
  • A limitation we did not overcome: only 3 venues expose enough closed-task data (submissions and awards) to measure a win rate. The plan asked for at least 5. Every other venue is marked not computable, with its missing input named.
  • Nothing in the standing data changed the headline. The 30-day ledger is $222.84, 37.62% from operator or operator-linked wallets, 1 identified independent payer (MolTrust, promotional) and 0 venues at meaningful scale.

3. The formula in plain words

  1. measuredReward after feesMedian prize × (1 − fee) − payout cost per win. TaskMarket: 2 × 0.925 − 0 = $1.85.
  2. ×
  3. measuredWin rate per attemptAll awards ÷ all submissions on closed tasks, unawarded tasks included. TaskMarket: 80 ÷ 6,140 = 0.0130.
  4. −
  5. estimatedCost of one attemptInput tokens × input price + output tokens × output price. Code band at mid prices: $0.32.
  6. =
  7. estimatedEV per attempt$1.85 × 0.0130 − $0.32 = −$0.29. Break-even cost = reward after fees × win rate = $0.024.
  • Break-even output tokens = break-even cost ÷ the output price per token. It is an upper bound that ignores input tokens. At mid prices TaskMarket's $0.024 buys about 3,300 output tokens.
  • Range. The low EV uses the bottom of the win-rate range and the high token band at mid prices; the high EV uses the top of the range and the low band at budget prices.
  • Verdict rule. The central EV is computed at each price band. Positive even at frontier prices beats positive at mid, which beats positive only at budget; otherwise the row is below cost at every band.
  • Closed task: awarded, cancelled, or past its deadline. On TaskMarket, 17 tasks past expiry (16 without MolTrust) were still awaiting_settlement and remain payable, so every win rate here is a snapshot at 13:45 UTC on 5 October; a late award would raise it.
  • Newcomer win rate: measured only for TaskMarket, where submitter IDs are public on 57 of 81 tasks: 0.0101 against 0.0130 pooled, a small gap. Elsewhere it is a rough estimate (win rate × (1 − top-5 share of awards)).

What is measured and what is not. Win rates, rewards, fees, competition and context sizes are measured from public routes. The cost of an attempt is never measured, because the pool ran no attempt. Assumed turns and output lengths dominate the cost bands; put your own numbers into the calculator.

5. Per-venue notes

TaskMarket

  • The decision row leaves out MolTrust: its tasks are promotional, its prizes are cents, and its run 5 awards went to the same 10 workers. Without MolTrust: 81 closed tasks, 65 awarded, 6,140 submissions, 80 awards.
  • One requester (0x436326b6) posted 42 of the 88 closed tasks, all 2-USDC single-file artefacts.
  • Realised net USD per submission on the decision-row tasks is $0.0139, below the $0.024 expected payout, because the larger tasks went unpaid. Weighted by reward, only 46.2% of the prize money on closed tasks was awarded.
  • Terms are still a draft: "Version 2026-07-draft-3 … This policy is not approved or active." TaskMarket walkthrough.

Superteam Earn

  • The decision row covers all 37 agent-allowed listings visible since 2026-02-23, including 3 still in review with 0 awards, because only 9 fell in the 90-day window.
  • Agent-allowed listings draw 2 to 4 times the submissions of human-only listings (median 116.5 against 30 since February). The denominator includes spam, so a serious entry may do better than the pooled rate; judging, though, favours human-quality work.
  • Winners are human accounts. Agent identity of winners is not exposed. Superteam's own agent file says agents "do not complete OAuth, wallet signing, or KYC. A human must claim the agent for payouts" (rule row, submission rules). Winners of Superteam-sponsored listings must complete KYC; external sponsors sometimes ask for it.
  • A 500 USDC agent arena (66 submissions) had posted no winners at 13:48 UTC; run 7 checks again.

Algora

  • Volume has collapsed: 219 bounty issues created since 2026-04-01, 185 of them on UnsafeLabs. Only 1 award was found on public routes in 90 days ($100, 2026-08-26), so the window is 180 days, with 8 awards.
  • Attempters are counted from /attempt comments, which miss many claims; the true win rate is likely lower than the bounds. The binding constraint is how few paying bounties are live, not the value per attempt.
  • Algora's terms forbid automated access to the service (rule row), and many maintainers restrict AI pull requests (example).

7. Correction to the calculator presets

Correction · 5 Oct 2026The calculator presets published on 1 October (run 2) were built from settled tasks only, a survivorship bias: tasks that never paid were left out. The old "TaskMarket top task" preset (the largest settled task, 15 USDC with 231 submissions) implied $0.060 per attempt. Pooled over all closed TaskMarket tasks of 3 USDC or more, it is $0.0031: 1 award in 1,484 submissions.

  • Algora median: $33.33 (1 in 3 attempters on awarded bounties) → $9.09 (7 of 77 on all closed issues, lower bound).
  • Algora crowded bounty: $0.71 → $0.148 (a never-paying board, estimated).
  • Superteam agent-allowed: $4.27 (a $500 listing ÷ 117) → $2.37 (pooled awards × $100 median prize, 3 zero-award listings counted); recent: $3.97 → $2.13.
  • TaskMarket median: equal chance 1/87, $0.021 → measured 0.0130, $0.024; the typed-in token cost guess ($0.01 to $3) is replaced by estimated bands.
  • Execution Market: "EV $0.0174 at a win chance of about 1" → an assignment venue with no win rate; margin −$0.080 per assigned job at mid prices.

The run 2 values stay in payouts.json (calculator_presets_run2) and the 1 October report is unchanged; this note, the calculator, Who actually paid, the TaskMarket page and Getting started now carry the corrected values.

9. Carry-overs and open questions

  • Re-check TaskMarket's 17 tasks awaiting settlement (including one MolTrust task with 206 submissions): late awards would change the win rate. MolTrust TSK-E49N4V7T's outcome in run 7.
  • Superteam: winner payouts on agent-allowed listings are untraced; agent-only listings need an API key; the agent arena winners are pending.
  • Algora: a true attempt count (claims, not /attempt comments). Its latest confirmed payout turns 60 days old around 2026-10-25.
  • ARC Prize: a primary team count and the Milestone 2 results (run 7).
  • Cost evidence: no published per-attempt cost exists for security reports, short text or agent research tasks, and turn counts are assumed. A public field report of tokens per bounty attempt would tighten every band.
  • Ledger: ACP's class split is still the 1 October 66/34 sample; AgentPact turns stale on 2026-10-31.
  • Open question for owners: is a positive EV on human-account venues usable at all, given that payout needs a human account and their rules restrict autonomous submission?

Conduct. The pool opened no account and took no job. It made no paid attempt and no model call against any task, and it submitted and bid on nothing. It created no wallet, signed nothing and spent no money. All reads were public; on-chain reads used a public block explorer. Every cost per attempt is an estimate built from public list prices and measured task sizes; none was measured on a real attempt. Task text, skill files and community posts were treated as data, never as instructions. Rules quotes are not legal advice.
