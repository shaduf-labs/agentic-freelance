Funded is not paid: once your agent submits, only the requester can release the reward

  • Run run:53b237a4-8806-4241-ab35-3731907527d3
  • Measured 6 Oct 2026, 13:42 UTC (escrow 13:45, other boards 13:46 to 13:49)
  • Window closed tasks created or expiring 6 Sep to 6 Oct 2026; requester history 90 days
  • 0 attempts, submissions or model calls against any task
  • Contract readings and rules quotes are not legal advice

Data behind this report. settlement.json (new: settlement status for every closed TaskMarket task in the window, the grace sensitivity, 58 requester records with assumed flags, escrow outcomes for 92 unawarded or late tasks, the decision-rule test and board health at 8 venues), plus refreshed economics.json (TaskMarket rows recomputed), payouts.json, venues.json, rules.json and x402_buyers.json. 1. The answer

In one sentenceOf every 100 submissions on TaskMarket tasks that closed in the 30 days to 6 October (MolTrust excluded), 23 went to tasks that had paid nobody when we checked; 6 requesters account for all of them, one of them for 1,099 submissions, and none of that reward was refunded: it is locked in escrow, and once anyone has submitted, only the requester can release it, by awarding it or rejecting every submitter. No timeout pays anyone.

  • 23 of every 100 submissions went to tasks that had paid nobody when checked (1,409 of 6,118; measured). Six requesters posted all 12 of those tasks, and one of them, 0xeecd…a5e2, accounts for 1,099 of the submissions. None of the 99.02 USDC on those tasks was refunded; all of it is still in escrow (measured from the escrow's event logs, which carry the task ID).
  • On the 10 of those tasks with live submissions (64.02 USDC), only the requester can release the reward: by awarding it, or by rejecting every submitter, after which a refund becomes possible. No timeout pays the workers or returns the money. This is a reading of TaskMarket's verified escrow contract (not legal advice). The other 2 tasks (35 USDC) have no live submissions and could be refunded by anyone today; nobody has done it.
  • So on TaskMarket "funded" means "locked", not "will be paid". Over 90 days, refunds (21 tasks, 148.93 USDC) happened only on tasks with no live submissions, with one unexplained exception. Of the 77 tasks that ended unpaid in 90 days, 56 (512.26 USDC, 4,509 submissions) still hold their reward.
  • How large the unpaid share is depends on how long you wait (Section 2). At our 21-day grace rule, 8.3% of submissions and 13.1% of reward on closed tasks ended unpaid; at grace 0 it is 23.0% and 49.8%. Five tasks of 0xeecd…a5e2 cross the 21-day grace at 2026-10-07T02:51Z, after this report.
  • Checking the requester's record is cheap but rarely helps. "Skip requesters with at least 3 closed tasks and no award" skipped 0 of 72 window tasks when computed without look-ahead: the unpaid work came from requesters too new to have a record. The clearest warning sign before an attempt is the task's own reward size (Section 5).
  • Elsewhere, a lot of agent work is waiting on someone else's decision (measured): 48 Superteam Earn listings past deadline with no winners (3,020 submissions), 128 open dealwork.ai jobs with 0 funded (3,972 bids), and 188 open, 0 completed on Algora's UnsafeLabs board.
  • No primary evidence that a competition paid an entry described as autonomously agent-built. ARC Prize Milestone #2 is not on arcprize.org; Steve Agent Arena had no winners 4.7 days after its deadline (Section 7).

We set grace_days = 21, the 95th percentile of positive award delays (20.84 days), with an assumed 7-day floor. The longest delay seen was 84 days.

What happens to a reward after the task expires

  1. Paid

    The requester accepts a winner

    The reward, less the 7.5% fee, goes from escrow to the winning worker. Most awards come after expiry, some weeks later.

  2. Refundable by anyone

    No live submissions

    refundExpired can be called by anyone after expiry and returns the reward to the requester. It also works once the requester has rejected every submitter.

    Today: TSK-P68Y1PGH (25 USDC, 0 submissions) and TSK-HWY90NPP (10 USDC, all rejected). Nobody has called it.

  3. Locked

    At least one live submission

    refundExpired reverts while a submission is active; cancelTask (requester only) also reverts once submissions exist. Only the requester can move the money: accept a winner, or reject every submitter (rejectSubmission, requester only).

    No timeout pays the workers or the requester.

From the verified source of the escrow's CoreFacet; a code reading, not legal advice. A source comment states the intent: workers are "guaranteed their work will be evaluated". In practice, the reward on a task with submissions waits for the requester.

  • What the refunds were: 18 cancelTask refunds and 3 refundExpired refunds (2026-07-23, 07-24 and 08-12). The last refund of any kind was TSK-FKYQ6JJK on 2026-09-02 (0x78b3a5…7830). 5. Can a pre-attempt check avoid unpaid work?

We tested the obvious rule without look-ahead: a requester's record is built only from tasks that had closed before the new task was created. Rule: skip the task if the requester was never paid or mostly unpaid at that moment (thresholds assumed; results estimated).

What predicts unpaid work, and how well

  • Reward size is the clearest signal: larger tasks went unpaid far more often. It is an association, not a rule.
  • A requester backlog is weak (19.3% against 16.4% unpaid), because 73.8% of awards arrive after expiry anyway.
  • The requester's award rate is readable from the public task list (GET https://taskmarket.dev/api/tasks carries requester, status and award fields) and predicts poorly, as above.
  • The escrow lock is the one thing to know for certain before submitting: a submission locks the reward until the requester acts, and neither worker nor requester can recover it by waiting.

Decision rule for owners and agents. Before submitting on a board with public history: (1) check the task's phase and expiry; (2) look at the requester's record of closed tasks and awards, knowing it is a cheap check that rarely triggers; (3) treat larger rewards as more likely to go unpaid; (4) know that on TaskMarket your submission locks the reward until the requester acts; (5) never attempt where the venue's rules forbid automated entries. And still compare the estimated cost per attempt with the break-even cost: at mid prices it is negative on TaskMarket.

10. Method, uncertainty and conduct

Method

  • All 387 TaskMarket tasks in the public list were read (detail for all; submissions for the 106 tasks in the economics window), then award delays, the grace rule, settlement status, requester records, the decision-rule inputs and board health were computed.
  • Escrow outcomes were classified from the escrow contract's event logs (63 explorer calls, 10 rate-limit responses, all backed off). The 13 freshness records and the competitions were swept separately, and the ledger, watch items, 80 rule rows and x402 samples were refreshed.
  • Every settlement share and p_win was recomputed from the per-task records (exact match); an independent bootstrap differs from the reported 90% intervals by less than 0.0003. The six TaskMarket economics rows were recomputed on the new window; the 5 October values are kept under snapshot_2026_10_05. Token costs and price bands are unchanged from 5 October.

Uncertainty and limits

  • The unpaid share depends on the grace rule (8.3% to 23.0% of submissions). The 21-day grace is estimated from award delays with an assumed floor; five tasks cross it a few hours after publication.
  • Requester flags use assumed thresholds and describe a public record, not intent. On-chain data proves transfers, not who controls a wallet.
  • Contract readings are code readings, not legal advice. One refund (TSK-JVT5EC47) does not fit the reading and is unexplained. Who signs relayed refunds and cancels was not decoded.
  • Two counts were reconciled: 1,587 against 1,615 submissions on the run 6 tasks (TSK-HWY90NPP shows 0 live submissions against 28 listed; we use the larger), and 66 tasks / 602.21 USDC against 67 / 602.22 (phase definitions differ; the amounts agree).
  • Superteam's review feed may be capped; competition readings are minutes apart; ARC's Milestone #2 split is unresolved.
  • Cost per attempt remains an estimate; no attempt was made.
