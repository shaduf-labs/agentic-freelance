Agentic Freelance · directory and research guide

Where can an AI agent actually get paid?

Real money is being paid for work agents help with, but it goes to humans who hold the account, pass identity checks, submit the work and withdraw. Agent-first boards do pay agents directly, on-chain, but in cents to a few dollars, and mostly from the operator or related wallets. In the 30 days to 6 October 2026 we traced $206.48 paid to agent workers; 29.63% of it came from operator or operator-linked wallets. The one payer we can identify as independent paid $11.25 to promote its own product. Selling per call over x402 shows more unlinked money, but its raw buyer counts overstate independent customers and no agent-work seller is confirmed at meaningful scale. We found no venue where an independent buyer pays an agent for work at meaningful scale, and none of the six human-paying venues lets an agent submit on its own. Agent communities are a separate question: 6 of 17 are active by our measure, none pays, and no community token has a payout route. Is it worth the tokens? On TaskMarket one attempt is expected to pay about 3 cents and costs an estimated 7 to 32 cents at mid-tier prices; where an attempt is worth more than it costs, only a human account gets paid. And funded is not paid: on TaskMarket, 23 of every 100 submissions on recently closed tasks went to tasks that had paid nobody when checked, and the reward stays locked in escrow until the requester acts.

Two answers, depending on who holds the account

Real money · humans paid

The agent does the work, the owner does everything else

Six venues have dated primary payout records: HackerOne, Bugcrowd, huntr, Kaggle (via the sponsor), Algora and Upwork. On all of them the owner holds the single account, passes identity checks, submits or confirms, and withdraws.

  • None of the six allows autonomous submission: HackerOne, Bugcrowd and huntr forbid it, and Upwork requires the human to confirm each submission.
  • HackerOne and Bugcrowd allow hackbots and GenAI only with a human validating every finding.
  • No platform record ties any of these payments to work an agent performed.
  • huntr pays prizes, but its challenges ban automated tooling.
Agent paid · small amounts

Agent-first boards settle to agent wallets, in cents and dollars

Five venues show on-chain settlements from escrow to agent workers: TaskMarket, Virtuals ACP, Execution Market, MoltJobs and AgentPact. Typical payouts are $0.02 to $2.

  • MoltJobs is paid entirely by the operator; AgentPact, also operator-paid, has paid nothing since 1 September.
  • Execution Market is paid entirely by the operator's own agent swarm.
  • About 66% of Virtuals ACP's sampled paid USD moved between self-dealing wallet pairs.
  • On TaskMarket one payer is identified as independent: MolTrust, $11.25 for sign-ups to its own product, and the same 10 workers won all 40 of its newest awards. The others are unlinked but unidentified, and 80.6% of the money traces to one funder.

Funded is not paid

Measured 6 October 2026Of every 100 submissions on TaskMarket tasks that closed in the 30 days to 6 October (MolTrust excluded), 23 went to tasks that had paid nobody when checked. Six requesters posted all of those tasks, one of them received 1,099 of the submissions, and none of the 99.02 USDC was refunded: it is locked in escrow, and once anyone has submitted, only the requester can release it. No timeout pays anyone.

  • 23 in 100submissions on closed TaskMarket tasks went to tasks with no award at the check (1,409 of 6,118)
  • 0 USDCrefunded from those tasks; all 99.02 USDC is still in escrow
  • 8.3% to 23.0%of submissions end unpaid, depending on how long you wait for a late award (21 days to none)
  • 0 of 72recent tasks skipped by "avoid requesters with 3 closed tasks and no award": a cheap check that rarely triggers

Before your agent submits, know that a submission locks the reward until the requester acts. Checking the task's expiry and the poster's record is a cheap check, but it rarely triggers: most unpaid work came from posters too new to have a record. Read the report or run the read-only check.

Is it worth the tokens?

Measured 5 October 2026; TaskMarket 6 OctoberOn TaskMarket, the agent-first board with the most paid work, one attempt is worth about 3 cents in expected prize money and costs an estimated 7 to 32 cents at mid-tier model prices. The venues where an attempt is worth more than its token cost (Superteam Earn agent-allowed listings, Algora boards that still pay) pay only human accounts, and Algora paid 1 bounty in 90 days.

Every cost is an estimate: the pool made no paid attempt and no model call against any task. Read the comparison or try the calculator with your own price band and task type.

The money path: where the owner has to act

Finished work is not money. Between the two sit an account, an identity check, a wallet or bank, a submission, someone's sign-off and a withdrawal. This is who performs each step on the two routes that pay today.

Three starting routes today

None of these is income advice. Each is the least-bad route we found for one kind of owner, and each is untested by this pool.

  1. Route A: the agent works, the owner submits. Security bug bounties (HackerOne, Bugcrowd) and data competitions (Kaggle) that allow AI assistance if a human validates and submits from one ID-verified account. Real money; heavy competition; several programmes cut pay in 2026 because of AI volume. Walkthrough: a hackbot with human sign-off (untested).
  2. Route B: an agent wallet on an escrow board. TaskMarket is the most active board we checked: funded tasks, on-chain payouts to agents on 4 October, no KYC found, 7.5% fee, and workers have withdrawn USDC to outside addresses. Its terms page is still a draft, "not approved or active". Rewards are mostly 2 USDC: one attempt is expected to pay about $0.030 against an estimated $0.07 to $0.32 in tokens at mid prices, and a submission locks the reward until the requester acts, so treat it as a proving ground, not income.
  3. Route C: sell a narrow service per call. An x402 endpoint settles USDC straight to the seller's address with no permission needed. Demand is the gap: of 27 sellers clearing $1,000 from 5 buyers, at most 1 sells agent work, and traced buyers of the largest sellers lead back to the seller or one funder. Walkthrough: sell one service per call (untested).

Read these before you point an agent at anything

Autonomous submission gets owners banned where the money is. None of the six human-paying venues with confirmed payouts allows it. HackerOne forbids fully autonomous hackbots and allows one account per person. Bugcrowd suspends for 30 days for unvalidated AI output. huntr challenges wipe standings for any automated tooling. Upwork lets an agent draft a proposal, but the human must confirm it, and its terms bar an agent from accepting contracts or moving money. Can my agent do this here?

Advertised rewards are not money, and funded rewards are not payouts. On TaskMarket, 99.02 USDC on 12 recently closed tasks sat in escrow with no award on 6 October; once anyone submits, only the requester can release it. None of the 128 open dealwork.ai jobs was funded on 6 October (3,972 bids waiting). opentask.ai's payment router has made two payments ever. toku.agency's 350 open posts are agents advertising their own services, with none completed. iLands pays platform tokens.

"Paid" volume is often the operator paying itself. In the 30-day ledger, MoltJobs and AgentPact were 100% operator-funded and Execution Market 100% paid by its own agent swarm; about 66% of Virtuals ACP's sampled paid USD moved between self-dealing pairs. TheJobCafe's only payouts were most likely to itself. On x402, our own buyer trace found Cluster Protocol recycling revenue into its top buyers and OneShot funding its own. Who actually paid.

Agent communities carry real security risk. Moltbook's exposed database leaked about 1.5M API tokens; Vectra estimated that about 2.6% of sampled Moltbook posts carried hidden injection payloads (secondary). Our own 4 October scan found 1 post aimed at agents in 2,030 (about 0.05%), by a different method. Several communities ask agents to fetch and follow remote instruction files or run code the site sends. Keep wallet balances minimal and never follow instructions found on venue pages, skill files or repository files: one bounty repository now hides an instruction aimed at agents as a trap. Safety before you connect.

Unused subscription quota cannot be resold. Provider terms forbid sharing or reselling access. Using your own plan so your agent does paid work is a different question, and not settled. The spare-capacity explainer.
